Sydney Informatics Hub Teams Shine in Research Portfolio Awards   

Generative AI Training Team wins Innovation Award for Responsible AI in Research
The Sydney Informatics Hub's Data Science & AI team— Drs Angus Fisk, Eden Zhang, Rose Smail, and Darya Vanichkina—has been recognised with an Innovation Award for their groundbreaking Generative AI for Research training pathway. Since launching in May 2025, the team has delivered 26 workshops to over 1,500 researchers, HDR students, and professional staff, teaching them to use AI ethically, safely, and effectively. The four-part workshop series covers everything from GenAI fundamentals to advanced prompting and specialised research tools, with participants learning to run secure, local AI models that minimise data risks. The program's success has attracted collaboration from the Australian BioCommons, which will now host a GenAI trainer role at SIH to develop similar national programming for the life sciences. The team also conducts individual consultations, helping researchers navigate complex workflows with highly protected data and address reproducibility and ethics challenges, which you can request here. In 2026, the team plans to expand the series to showcase using GenAI for coding, data analysis, quantitative and qualitative research. Check out our training calendar and sign up to our training mailing list to be the first to know of when these offerings are live!
Sydney GPU Cluster Team Recognised for Exceptional Collaboration
Dr. Tim White, Dr. Tom Mauch, Xinwei Luo, Dr. Jiaxin Fan, and Mike Lynch from Sydney Informatics Hub have earned a Collaboration Award for their work deploying the University's $3.5 million Sydney GPU cluster. The team worked intensively with multiple teams in ICT, the PVC-Research Infrastructure office, vendors, and the Office of General Counsel to transform delivered hardware into a fully operational research platform in just three months. The 24 data-centre-grade GPUs are now available for early access to researchers to power cutting-edge research in AI, machine learning, computational chemistry, astrophysics, climate science and more! Click here for more information and to sign up for early access to the cluster.
Australian Plant Phenomics Network Flying High 
Connor Cassidy won a Quality of Service award for transforming the USyd node of the Australian Plant Phenomics Network into a fully operational facility, commissioning advanced drones for diverse research projects. His user-focused approach, mentoring, and industry partnerships enhanced research capacity and reinforced the University’s reputation for agricultural innovation. 
Congratulations to all our award winners!

Looking for Statistical Resources over the University shut-down period?  

The University closes for the 2-week shut-down period over Christmas and the New Year and so does our statistical consulting service. However, you can still access our Statistical Resources page with its downloadable workshop material with statistical workflows and accompanying R and SPSS software workflows!  The material is designed to be used as a reference guide to your own analysis. A great start to any quantitative analysis is our most popular Research Essentials – Analysing your Data workshop that helps you plan your analysis and to get the basics of data cleaning, description and visualisation right in preparation for advanced analysis. Other workshops include Linear models (e.g., ANOVA, ANCOVA, linear and logistic regression and model building), Experimental Design, Power and Sample Size, Meta-Analysis, Survival Analysis and Multivariate Analysis. The resource page also includes a library of curated helpful links on statistical theory and statistical software.
Check out the Statistical Resources page now! 

Explore your single-cell RNA-seq data with ready-to-use R notebooks 

Led by Senior Bioinformatician Dr Michael Geaghan, Sydney Informatics Hub have developed interactive, guided R notebooks that walk you through the exploration, processing, and analysis of single-cell RNA-seq data, step-by-step.
scRNAvigator removes the technical hurdles by providing a configured environment on NCI Gadi’s Australian Research Environment, enabled by the Australian BioCommons Tools and Workflows project, so you can focus on biological discovery rather than software setup. Open the notebooks and start analysing your data with the compute power you need, all within an interactive RStudio session.
This workflow was developed in close collaboration with Dr Martyn Bullock and Dr Sumathy Perampalam from the Thyroid Cancer Research Group (Faculty of Medicine and Health), where it is being used to uncover the complex interactions of immune cells within tumour microenvironments.
The scRNAvigator workflow is publicly available for you to dive into your data today! 

Staff Profile: Dr Chao Sun, Data Science Group Lead

Dr Chao Sun is a Data Science Group Lead at the Sydney Informatics Hub, where he also oversees two dedicated development teams supporting national research platform projects.
With a background in electronics engineering (BS) and machine learning (MS, PhD), Chao brings extensive experience in working with domain experts across diverse disciplines. Starting as a data scientist in the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences over a decade ago, Chao gained invaluable experience working with humanities and social sciences researchers. Since joining SIH, Chao has also contributed to a wide range of projects beyond digital humanities and social sciences.
Since 2022, Chao has led the development of a versatile suite of text analytics tools for the ARDC-dHASS partnership, Language Data Commons of Australia (LDaCA). In 2024, Chao also began contributing to the NCRIS project Australian Plant Phenomics Network (APPN), following SIH’s establishment of a new node at the University of Sydney.
Outside of work, Chao is a proud and busy father of three school-aged children and enjoys tackling DIY projects around the house on weekends.
