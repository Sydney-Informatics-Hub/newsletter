SydneyFMS: New

University-wide

facility booking platform

coming soon 

This year, the University will introduce SydneyFMS (Facility Management System), a new platform that is set to provide a single point of visibility and access to research facilities, equipment and services across approximately 20 different areas of the University. SydneyFMS will be introduced in stages, gradually replacing the various legacy booking systems used by these facilities.
SIH will be among the first facilities to transition to SydneyFMS. At this point in time, the change will only apply to SIH users booking our CLC Genomics and IPA software. To facilitate the transition, there will be a planned outage of our current software booking system. Software bookings will temporarily close from 11:59pm Friday 4 April, and will re-open in SydneyFMS at 9am Tuesday 8 April. Usage data and future-dated bookings will be transferred to the new system. The new system uses the same platform as our current system, so while there will be some additional features it will feel familiar. 
For further information, see the SydneyFMS intranet page.

Last week, SIH hosted the first Australian outpost of the global nf-core hackathon with Australian BioCommons. We had the pleasure of welcoming friends from across the country to our Sydney office for 3 days of Nextflow flavoured bioinformatics.
We connected with global efforts to add new 3D protein prediction models to the nf-core/proteinfold pipeline, including Alphafold3, Boltz, and Helixfold3. Alexandra (Ali) Green is a Statistical Consultant at the Sydney Informatics Hub. She has been working in applied statistics since 2020, completing a postdoctoral fellowship in veterinary epidemiology and statistics, following a PhD in cattle bioacoustics.
Since joining the Sydney Informatics Hub in 2022, Ali has appreciated consulting across a wide range of disciplines, including medical sciences, arts and linguistics, psychology, engineering, and business. Introduction to

Survival Analysis

30 April Hybrid

workshop: in-person or Zoom

Across the University of Sydney researchers in different disciplines are studying the time elapsed up to when a specified event occurs. For example, you might be interested in factors that influence survival of a person after a disease diagnosis or survival of a company after a stock market crash. Perhaps you like to study the survival time of marriage, or you are interested in how long a device or manufacturing component lasts before it fails or becomes obsolete. Survival analysis has also been applied to study staff churn or promotion, and the optimum time for soccer player substitution during a game.
To learn more about how ‘time-to event’/ survival analysis works, and how you can incorporate it into your research projects, sign up to the free Sydney Informatics Hub ‘Introduction to Survival Analysis’ workshop happening on April 30.

Meta-Analysis: an introduction

30 April Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

Are you currently working on a systematic review or plan to do so in the future? If yes, then it is important for you to know about meta-analysis, a statistical technique that can add a valuable quantitative component to your qualitative literature review. In contrast to primary studies that combine information across individual subjects, meta-analysis combines information across multiple studies.
This powerful technique can provide a more precise estimate of the quantitative effect of a particular phenomenon, whether it be an educational intervention, drug treatment, disease incidence in the human population, or anything else. In addition to increased accuracy, meta-analysis provides a quantitative snapshot of the existing research in your field, enabling you to compare the contribution of individual studies to an overall estimate. You can use the technique to better understand conflicting results across multiple studies by examining potential publication bias, and the effect of different population or study subgroups.
To learn more about how meta-analysis works, and how you can incorporate it into your research projects, sign up to the free Sydney Informatics Hub Meta-Analysis workshop happening on April 30.

Building Interpretable Models to tell your

research story: Linear Models 3

30 April Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

Statistical analysis is more than just building the best predictive model, it should also enable researchers to make the discoveries required to build new knowledge. Constructing engaging narratives about their research is also invaluable as researchers look to connect with their field, the community and funding bodies. To do this researchers need to test hypotheses, uncover unknown patterns, and present results in insightful, intuitive and memorable ways. In short, researchers need to build interpretable models. Our Linear Models 3 workshop to be delivered on 30 April explores tips and tricks to make your statistical analyses do just that.
Topics covered will be:
  • Reporting tricks that aid interpretation - estimated marginal means, confidence vs prediction intervals, applying and correcting for multiple comparisons, reporting variable ‘importance’, plus other reporting and interpretation tricks
  • Model Parameterisation using the Design Matrix - interpreting categorical predictor parameters, dummy and effects coding
  • More on Mixed Models - introducing the random slope
Check out our training calendar for more information and to register.
