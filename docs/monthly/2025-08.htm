Introducing New SIH Staff Member: Dr Senhui (Alex) Guo, Data Science Software Engineer, Australian Text Analytics Platform (ATAP)

Dr Senhui (Alex) Guo has recently joined SIH as a Data Science Software Engineer working on the Language Data Commons of Australia (LDaCA), where he leverages his expertise in Generative AI to enhance cutting-edge technologies for text analysis. He is passionate about advancing the ATAP project and empowering university researchers with advanced tools and insights.
Currently he’s working on rebuilding the text analytics user interface with a full-stack web app to offer more functionalities and a modern UI design. The backend is built with fastAPI, frontend with React. The underlying text analysis tools are built on top of polars, a super fast python library to handle dataframes.
He’s also actively utilising AI agent to help him write the codebase. With the latest developments of agents and MCPs, he’s considering integrating these technologies into the text analysis platform to offer a code-free analysis experience. Welcome, Alex!

Staff Profile: Dr William (Tam) Salter, University of Sydney APPN Node Director

Dr William (Tam) Salter is a Lecturer in Crop Physiology in the School of Life and Environmental Sciences at The University of Sydney. Based at the University's expansive field research station in Narrabri, northern NSW, he leads a dynamic research group within the International Centre of Crop and Digital Agriculture. His work focuses on developing and deploying innovative phenotyping methodologies to better understand plant physiological responses to environmental stressors, with the goal of enhancing agroecosystem resilience and productivity.
Tam leads The University of Sydney node of the Australian Plant Phenomics Network (APPN), which provides national access to cutting-edge phenotyping infrastructure and expertise. The Sydney node offers advanced field-based platforms including drone-mounted hyperspectral, multispectral, high resolution RGB, LiDAR and, soon, thermal imaging systems. They also offer access to high-throughput canopy analysis tools, plant physiology instrumentation, integrated environmental monitoring systems and proximal soil sensing equipment. These resources support a wide range of collaborative research projects across academia and industry.
Tam is passionate about translating fundamental plant science into practical solutions for agriculture, and actively engages with growers, breeders, and technology developers to ensure impact beyond the lab.

Do you need a second opinion? 

The Sydney Informatics Hub Statistical Consulting Unit is occasionally asked for a second opinion on a data analysis. In each of two recent similar cases, a researcher approached the team with what they thought was a near-complete analysis, only to find that their analysis - undertaken by an inexperienced statistician - was far below publication standard. In both cases, Alexandra Green and Alex Shaw from the Statistical Consulting Unit were able to reanalyse the data and rescue the analysis. Although these cases are relatively rare, they highlight how poor methodological choices can jeopardise a timely high-impact publication.
How you do avoid the cost of a poorly-conducted analysis without having statistical expertise yourself? Think of a data analyst like a car mechanic. Having some basic statistical knowledge is like knowing the basics of car maintenance—it empowers you in two key ways:  
1) Do-it-yourself confidence: If you understand the fundamentals, you can handle simple tasks yourself. In the garage, that might mean checking the oil or changing a tyre. In data analysis, it could mean choosing the right plot to show a correlation or spotting a basic error in a dataset. Our modular 90-minute statistical workshops and embedded practical workflows will give you all the skills you need.
2) Choosing and trusting the expert: When the job is too complex—like rebuilding an engine or analysing clinical longitudinal data—you may need an expert. But with some foundational knowledge, you’ll be better equipped to pick a qualified analyst, ask the right questions, and understand whether the work has been done properly. Approach us for a consult to discuss your analysis requirements, and how we can help you.
With over 60 years of combined statistical experience in the Statistical Consulting Unit, we can deliver the high-quality support you need to achieve your analysis goals. Safe driving!

Our statisticians are coming to Camden!  

27 August Hybrid workshops: in-person or Zoom at Camden - all welcome!

Sydney Informatics Hub's Statistical Consulting Unit (SCU) are excited to return to the Camden campus on Wednesday 27 August for an informal drop-in session and to deliver three workshops.
Pop into Room 305 in the Centre for Carbon, Food and Water between 3:30pm and 4:30pm to ask any questions regarding your research data, study design or statistical analysis – no registration required.
The workshops earlier in the day will focus on statistical modelling and give you practical workflows for your own analysis. We will demystify the terms linear and logistic regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA, repeated measures and mixed models and show how all of these align in the one Linear Models framework that can be applied to build more complex models with multiple predictors.
