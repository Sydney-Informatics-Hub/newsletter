Training mini-series: making sense of gene and protein lists with functional enrichment analysis 

Last month, we rounded out our Functional Enrichment Analysis training mini-series with a hands-on bioinformatics workshop with Monash Genomics and Bioinformatics platform. Functional enrichment analysis is used to identify the biological signals within your ‘omics datasets. Following our introductory webinar, attendees from institutions across the country tried out some commonly used web and R-based tools for over representation analysis (ORA), gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) to identify enriched biological functions.
If you’re keen to learn about the statistics that underpin enrichment analyses, and be guided through the databases and tools available, you can watch the recording of our webinar online and run through the workshop materials. 

SIH joins Australian Outpost of BioHackathon Europe 

SIH members Fred Jaya and Tim White joined a diverse gathering of Australian researchers in Brisbane to participate in ELIXIR’s BioHackathon Europe 2024. Australian participation in the event is thanks to the ongoing collaboration between Australian BioCommons and ELIXIR.
Fred and Tim joined with team members from Australian BioCommons, Children’s Cancer Institute, the University of Melbourne, as well as European collaborators, to develop a prototype data model converter. This tool maps between common biomedical data formats, with an interactive and user-friendly web application to allow cohort selection and data conversion. With further development, the data model converter promises to accelerate biomedical research and enhance collaboration by providing researchers with an efficient means to work with data from disparate sources. 

SIH attends eResearch Australasia  

Members of the Sydney Informatics Hub attended the eResearch Australasia Conference in Melbourne last month. It was a wonderful opportunity for us to connect with others working in the “Third Space” from universities and research organisations across Australia and New Zealand, including in data science, AI, research software engineering, high performance & cloud computing and data management. 
☁️ 🤖 Dr Darya Vanichkina, our Data Science & AI Group Lead, led a panel discussion on how researchers are partnering with QCIF, Intersect, The University of Melbourne, Monash, The University of Sydney and UNSW to use cloud services for applications that make use of artificial intelligence. 
🗞️ 👩‍💻 Mike Lynch, our Software Engineering Group Lead, presented talks about the work SIH is undertaking to enhance the University’s digital humanities capabilities. 
🔭 👽🪐 Dr Tim White, one of our Data Science Software Engineers, took out the prize for best poster, presenting how we’re providing research support in the search for habitable planets with the Sydney-led TOLIMAN Space Telescope. 
Other conference highlights included talks on:
🎄 Australia’s amazing new Square Kilometre Array (SKA) – which looks a bit like a field of Christmas trees in the desert – and will generate 710 Petabytes of data per year – so lots of exciting challenges in figuring out how to analyse that much data fast!
🧬 How AlphaFold3, a deep-learning model which applies the latest AI techniques to predict how proteins fold, has revolutionised structural biology and is already leading to new ways to target treatments in clinical settings. 
💾 The new National Digital Research Infrastructure strategy and national computational infrastructure that forms part of it! 
If any of these sound exciting and you’d like to know more – please reach out to us at sih.info@sydney.edu.au! We’d love to catch up and talk more about any and all of the above (and more)! 

SIH Attends PyCon AU 2024 

Last weekend Xinwei Luo and Hamish Croser, two of SIH's data science software engineers, traveled to Melbourne to attend the Australian Python Conference (PyConAu). Hosted at the Melbourne Convention and Exhibition Centre, the event featured >100 presentations and had 500+ attendees!
The presentations covered a wide range of topics, including MicroPython, Django, AI/ML, open-source development, and more. Speakers showcased projects, explained programming concepts, and demonstrated useful Python tools. Highlights included a demonstration on building your own IoT with Raspberry Pi and Python, an explanation of how AI interprets human language using embeddings, and a showcase of napari, an n-dimensional image viewer for Python computer vision projects.
Each day concluded with lightning talks—5-minute presentations on any topic. These featured diverse subjects, such as Macquarie Island and its penguins, metaprogramming, a call for programming professionals to volunteer for state SES, and answering the question, "Is driving across Sydney faster than the NBN?" (Spoiler: yes). 

Professor Monika Bednarek elected as a new fellow of the Australian Academy of the Humanities 

We are delighted to share that Professor Monika Bednarek has been elected as a Fellow of the Australian Academy of the Humanities—one of the highest honors in the field. As the founding director of the Sydney Corpus Lab, Professor Bednarek is widely celebrated for her outstanding contributions to corpus linguistics, discourse analysis, and the study of language use in socially significant contexts.
The Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) has proudly collaborated with Professor Bednarek for many years, supporting the development of various corpus linguistic tools and platforms. Since 2021, SIH has been a partner in the national digital humanities infrastructure platforms, LDaCa and ATAP, where Professor Bednarek serves as the academic lead at the University of Sydney. 
Please join us in congratulating Professor Monika Bednarek on this exceptional achievement! 

SIH Staff Profile: Dr Sahand Vahidnia, Senior Machine Learning Engineer

Sahand is a Senior Machine Learning Engineer on the Software Engineering team at the Sydney Informatics Hub. He completed his PhD at UNSW, where his research explored artificial intelligence, graph analysis, and clustering techniques. His machine learning interests range from computer vision and natural language processing to time series analysis, with a strong focus on engineering and software development. 
Since joining SIH in 2023, Sahand has contributed to projects at the forefront of AI and software engineering, including the GRDC Soil Water Nowcasting project where he developed a pipeline, automating environmental satellite data acquisition, processing, model training, and prediction. He also worked on the Urban Heat Island Detection, an open-source project, enabling aerial detection of urban features and climate zones. He is currently focused on a 3D pose estimation project in collaboration with the Faculty of Medicine & Health to monitor and detect ankle sprain for NBA players. 
Outside of work, he is an anime enthusiast and a trained oil painting artist. He has recently taken up motorcycle riding and enjoys exploring new routes on the weekends.
