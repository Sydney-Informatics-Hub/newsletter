Project Spotlight: Largest study of Australian online gambling suggests voluntary limits are ineffective

Associate Professor Sally Gainsbury and Dr. Robert Heirene of the University of Sydney’s Gambling Treatment & Research Clinic (GTRC) worked with SIH Data Scientist Dr Darya Vanichkina to carry out the largest study of consumer protection tool use among Australian online sports betters. Most online gambling customers did not use voluntary tools designed to limit problem betting, but policy changes requiring an “opt-out” approach nudged users to take up these tools. Furthermore, clients who requested timeouts from the wagering platforms placed more bets and in bigger amounts, had fewer days without gambling and had more variability in the amount they gambled and the amount they won from day to day – indicating they may represent a more vulnerable group that should be targeted with focused interventions. SIH enabled the scale and breadth of the analysis, with over 12 million wagers from 40 000 clients across 6 platforms analyzed using the University’s Artemis supercomputing cluster in support of these findings.

Read the paper here or the piece in University of Sydney News.

 

The National Compute Merit Allocation Scheme 2022 (NCMAS): Get help with applying

The National Computational Merit Allocation Scheme (NCMAS) 2022 call for applications will open 18 August 2021. This scheme will provide access to three of Australia’s largest high performance computing systems including the National Compute Infrastructure’s Gadi, the Pawsey Supercomputing Centre’s Setonix and MASSIVE’s M3 HPC.  

Support from the Sydney Informatics Hub

 The Sydney Informatics Hub are calling for expressions of interest from individuals and groups that would like direct support from the Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) in applying to NCMAS 2022. SIH are offering direct support by:  

  • Enabling scalability and parallelisation of your workflows
  • Obtaining preliminary results and benchmarking on National HPC facilities
  • Transitioning analysis and data processing workflows from Artemis HPC to National HPC facilities 
  • Preparing applications  

We will be limiting direct support to 10 research projects.  

How do I submit my expression of interest?  

If you would like support from the Sydney Informatics Hub for your NCMAS 2022 application, please fill in the request for project support form and: 

  • Include NCMAS 2022 in “Give this Request for Quote a title”
  • In “Brief description of your project”, please include:  
    • Grants supporting the project  
    • Overview of the software/workflows used 
    • HPC facilities you have used (e.g. Artemis HPC)

Help for working from home (again)

With the current lockdown extended and the end of the school holidays, there is a sense of deja vu, and many of us will be returning to working from home full time once more. It seems timely to (re)share some of the tips and resources to help you continue your research while away from campus. 
As research once again pivots away from face-to-face and back online the need to securely and safely communicate/share/collaborate/collect data will be on the minds of many. There are many resources available to help, from SIH and other support services at the university. 

  • Using Zoom and using zoom for research
  • Using the VPN
  • Access the RDS 
  • Send data securely using Cloudstor 
  • Collaborate using OneDrive and/or the eNotebook (don’t forget these extra eNotebook tips) 
  • Collect survey data using REDCap and/or Qualtrics
  • Watch our Digital Research Café video series 

    Most importantly, if you want to check that you are managing your research data in line with university requirements, or want to learn more about the above, get in contact with a Research Data Consultant. 

 

National Policy Development

The National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) is a national policy initiative which will provide more than $22 billion in funding a year for the next 5 years to Australians who have permanent and significant disability. The National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA) performed an evaluation of the pilot independent assessment of needs with Mary-Ann O'Donovan from the Centre for Disability Studies and Jen Smith-Merry from the Centre for Disability, Policy and Research asked to review and validate the NDIA evaluation. SIH assisted with some of the statistical issues associated with this review such as how to deal with missing data and the characteristics of the Shapley variable importance metric, particularly in the presence of multicollinearity.

SIH Project Scheme - Round 3 opening soon

Applications for the SIH Project Scheme - Round 3 will open Monday August 30th. The scheme aims to drive excellence in digital and data-driven research across the University by providing grants to support access to the SIH facility. SIH supports researchers with gaining more productive and new insights from their data using data science approaches, scientific computing infrastructure, and best practice data management.  
Apply for up to 12 full time equivalent weeks of support for your project (in-kind value of up to $33,600). SIH support includes conducting statistical analyses, designing surveys, data linkage, AI, machine learning, computer vision, data visualisation, text analytics, simulation, geospatial analysis, GPU programming, bioinformatics and more. Previous recipients have used this support towards manuscripts, reports, preliminary results for grant applications, and/or to progress their research. SIH support for projects awarded through this scheme would commence between October and December 2021. 

Applications close Sunday 19th of September.  
To apply for the scheme please complete this form. 
More information on the scheme will be available here. For assistance with preparing a competitive application or for further enquiries contact: sih.info@sydney.edu.au.

Wearable Technology Community Meetup

Wearable technologies are increasingly worn by consumers and provide a vital source of information for researchers. On July 20, the Sydney Informatics Hub will host the second Wearable Technology Community Event. If you work with, or intend to work with, data derived from wearable technologies, please come along.

Where to go when your bioinformatics outgrows your compute: 2 webinars

Bioinformatics analyses are often complex, requiring multiple software tools and specialised compute resources. “I don’t know what compute resources I will need”, “My analysis won’t run and I don’t know why” and "Just getting it to work" are common pain points for researchers.

The Sydney Informatics Hub are presenting two webinars to support life science researchers with their compute needs.

The first is on the 19th of August and is in partnership with the Australian BioCommons, Galaxy Australia, the Australian Research Data Commons, the National Compute Infrastructure and the Pawsey Supercomputing Centre: register for Where to go when your bioinformatics outgrows your compute.
Our second webinar is on the 20th of August and is for large scale, big data projects who wish to apply for the National Compute Merit Allocation Scheme in 2022: register for High performance bioinformatics: submitting your best NCMAS application
