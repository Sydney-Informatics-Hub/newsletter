Curated Collections – a New National Platform for Digital Humanities and Social Sciences  

Together with the Australian Research Data Commons’ Community Data Lab Project, Sydney Informatics Hub is leading the delivery of a new national service for creating digital collections and publishing them on the web with multimedia and interactive visualisations.
This project builds on a Research Portfolio Digital Infrastructure project in 2024 which gathered requirements from humanities researchers at the University of Sydney across a wide range of disciplines, from archaeology to literary theory.
The platform Omeka S allows researchers to create and publish digital collections using a self-service model, and the project will also develop training workshops and documentation to be delivered in 2026. You can see examples of collections created with Omeka S at their showcase.
The first release of the new platform will take place in November. Before then, you can register your interest in participating in the project by completing this EOI form. 

Sydney Informatics Hub GPU Cluster – Coming Soon! 

The Sydney Informatics Hub is standing up a new high-end GPU Cluster with high-speed storage for our research community. One of the first in Australia, the SIH NVIDIA DGX H200 cluster features 24 NVIDIA H200 GPU cards, each with 141GB VRAM. The potential for research acceleration across the University’s many research fields is enormous. This GPU cluster forms a part of our wider efforts to modernise the University’s research compute offering via the Sydney Research Cloud project, and is led by the Pro-Vice-Chancellor (Research Infrastructure). 
In anticipation of the new GPU cluster being researcher-ready by September, SIH are asking research leaders to start thinking and planning whether and how they might use this powerful new tool. The final access, services and charging model is still under active consultation. Similar principles to the other Core Research Facilities will be applied (i.e.) a key priority is our capacity to support the delivery of our community’s funded research projects. Guidelines for using the Sydney Research Infrastructure Access Grants to leverage this resource for proof-of-concept or demonstrator projects will also be provided soon.
Register your interest in accessing these new cutting-edge GPUs by filling this GPU EOI form so that we will contact you as we progressively roll out the system.
More information is available on the Research Computing @ Sydney confluence page here with some use cases for how you can leverage the GPU cluster in your research! 

Workshops taking off at Moore College

Since moving into our new offices at Moore College we’ve been making the most of its fabulous training facilities. Located on the top floor and boasting impressive views of the Sydney skyline, we’ve seen our in-person workshop numbers tracking upwards and are glad to report that we recently had a full house, with more people in person than on Zoom!
Having such an impressive training facility has certainly facilitated this return to in-person connections, with many researchers remarking on the inspiring surroundings, and choosing to return for subsequent workshops.
Not only is it much more energising and fun for us to present to a room full of people, making it a richer experience for everyone, including those joining virtually, but in-person attendees also benefit from a more dynamic and engaging learning experience by interacting with us and their peers directly when asking questions and during 2-way conversation. Research has shown can significantly enhance understanding compared to learning in isolation. 
We hope to see you at one of our workshops soon!

SIH Staff Profile: Dr Alex Shaw, Statistical Consultant

Alex is a Statistical Consultant at the Sydney Informatics Hub. After completing a PhD and a postdoc in medical research, Alex became interested in data analysis. Pursuing this interest, he found his ‘true calling’ as a statistician after completing a Master of Biostatistics.
He joined the statistical consulting team in 2019 and has helped develop many of the team’s research-applied learning resources that have empowered Sydney researchers from across every faculty of the university to overcome their statistical challenges. As a former researcher, Alex particularly enjoys being a ‘statistical mentor’ to early career researchers, building both their competence and confidence in all aspects of quantitative research. Over the years Alex has completed hundreds of one-on-one statistical consulting sessions and since the pandemic has encouraged a return to in-person consulting wherever possible.
Outside of work Alex enjoys music, as an audience member, a songwriter, occasional performer, and even more occasional archivist. He recently curated a pop-up exhibition to celebrate the release of his late father Kydric Shaw’s music from the 1970s. The exhibition included a poster, printed at Tin Sheds, for the Kydric Shaw Band’s first show, held at The Settlement. Both Tin Sheds and The Settlement are deeply connected to Sydney University history. After the exhibition, Alex and his son Tate also met another Sydney University institution, Dr Karl, who was a friend of Kydric Shaw during their respective music and taxi-driving years.

New SIH Generative AI workshops – coming to a Zoom session near you! 

In May, Sydney Informatics Hub piloted a new hands-on workshop on the use of AI in research, aimed at demystifying AI and getting participants up and running using it in their everyday work. The day-long workshop covered the fundamentals of what modern generative AI systems actually are, which ones you can use, and how to use them for academic work. Attendees walked out with the skills and tools to implement what they had learned. Run with a cohort of EMCRs from the Faculty of Medicine and Health at Camperdown and Westmead, generously supported by the Researcher Development Unit, we had enthusiastic attendance and engagement, with full rooms and incisive discussions that helped clear away some of the hype around AI.
The feedback was enthusiastic:
“Great workshop, highly recommend for anyone in medical research as we learn to navigate the new brave new AI world”

“Opened my eyes on how to "skillfully" use AI in my research.” 
For those outside of FMH or who were unable to attend, we will be delivering this content online, generalised to a wider profile of faculties, and split into 4 more digestible and expanded sessions (GENAI 101, 201, 202 and 203), regularly over the next few months.
Sign up to the first of these below and/or keep an eye out for additional sessions on our training calendar and newsletters. Thanks to everyone who joined at Westmead and Camperdown and made the days such a success! 
Sign up for the foundational workshop GENAI101 Fundamentals on 23 July, 10 am – 12 pm (online) or 24 July, 10 am – 12 pm (online).

Upcoming Machine Learning and Exploratory Data Analysis workshops in R and Python

We’re really excited to be offering our flagship Machine Learning and Exploratory Data Analysis training again in August and September this year.
We’re running Machine Learning and Exploratory Data Analysis courses in both R and Python soon. These workshops are designed to get you up to speed on modern data analysis tools to speed up your workflow and get you repeatable, reliable results as fast as possible. The Exploratory Data Analysis workshops will take you through the first steps of analysing and visualising your data and getting it in shape for whatever you need to do with it downstream. The Machine Learning workshops then supercharge your analysis by showing you how to use these powerful algorithms to find patterns and make predictions based on your data.
We are running these as online Zoom sessions: 
  • 26 August - Exploratory Data Analysis in Python 
  • 28 August - Machine Learning in Python 1 (Foundations and Regression) 
  • 29 August - Machine Learning in Python 2 (Classification, error metrics and unsupervised learning) 
  • 23 September - Exploratory Data Analysis in R
  • 25 September - Machine Learning in R 1 (Foundations and Regression)
  • 26 September - Machine Learning in R 2 (Classification, error metrics and unsupervised learning)
