ASKAP VAST Pipeline: Searching the radio sky

The Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder telescope (ASKAP) collects images of the sky at radio wavelengths with an unprecedented field of view, combined with a high sensitivity and fine angular resolution. The large quantity of data produced is used by the ASKAP Variables and Slow Transients survey (VAST) to study the dynamic radio sky, looking for interesting phenomena such as neutron star mergers and tidal disruption events in which a black hole rips apart a nearby star. Efficient pipelines are vital in this research, as we're looking for a ‘needle in a haystack’. 
Prof. Tara Murphy headed an international collaboration of astrophysicists with specialist support from SIH Data Science Software Engineer Dr. Sergio Pintaldi to develop the VAST pipeline, a modern and scalable software pipeline for processing radio telescope images. The pipeline is designed to look for radio transients, and includes a web app to interact with pipeline results, and tools to analyse and filter processed data to speed up the search for these events. The software is all open source, so you can check it out and even run it for yourself here.

 

REDCap Upgrade: REDCap 11.0.3

REDCap users may have noticed that there was a short service outage in June. This outage was due to a BIG upgrade, migrating from REDCap 10.0.1 to REDCap 11.0.3. With this upgrade comes a long list of new features, as well as many tweaks and fixes. Look out for improved field embedding, the ability to re-evaluate/re-activate Alerts and Automatic Survey Invitations, and many more new features.

Register for our webinar (12:30 – 13:30 14 July 2021) for a demonstration of some of the new and improved features and have your questions answered.

 

Computational Approaches to Language Research community 

As part of the Sydney Centre for Language Research, SIH coordinates a periodic meetup of researchers harnessing language and computation in research. Our meetings mostly attract researchers from Arts and Social Sciences, Medicine & Health, and Business. In April we heard about a web-based tool applied to analyse the language of ARC grant successes (Ian McCrabb), how online discussions around #MeToo identify power relationships (Paul Henry), and predicting the NZ vote on legalising cannabis using Google Trends data (Jacques Raubenheimer). Whether you’re new on the journey with natural language processing technologies, or an old hand, we’re keen to meet new faces. Get in touch with Joel Nothman at SIH if you’d like to join the mailing list.

 

Supporting the Faculty of Engineering Coding Fest, 2021

SIH and Intersect delivered the "Start Coding Without Hesitation: Programming Languages Showdown" course for the School of Computer Science, Faculty of Engineering on 9th June as part of the Coding Fest 2021. The short course showcased four of the most popular programming languages that are widely used in academia: Python, R, MATLAB, and Julia. We discussed the popularity of each language followed by a lively discussion of job opportunities. The general guidelines for starting programming were also well received by the attendees. The course was delivered in a hybrid mode, with 10 students attending the session in person and another 15 attended online.

Staff Profile

 

Henry Lydecker: Data Scientist

Originally from the USA, Henry has a background in environmental studies (BA, UC Santa Cruz) and ecology (PhD, Usyd). Before joining SIH, Henry’s research focused on parasite ecology and public health.  His areas of expertise include geospatial analysis, machine learning, natural language processing, and computer vision. At SIH, he has worked on projects from a range of domains, including agriculture, medicine, dentistry, ecology, and law. One of his most notable projects has been developing the Weed-AI open database of annotated crop imagery. 
Henry is very passionate about coffee, food, fitness, and photography. On a typical weekend you can find Henry out flying his drone taking photos somewhere along the coast, in between breaks for coffee.

Wearable Technology Community Meetup

Wearable technologies are increasingly worn by consumers and provide a vital source of information for researchers. On July 20, the Sydney Informatics Hub will host the second Wearable Technology Community Event. We bring together a diverse community of researchers using wearable devices in their research, spanning diabetes, architecture, psychology, sleep and other disciplines. By sharing knowledge and addressing common roadblocks, the community hopes to accelerate the time to science from research question to real world application. If you work with, or intend to work with, data derived from wearable technologies, please come along.
