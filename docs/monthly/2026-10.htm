Apollo GPU Cluster Launch   

The Apollo GPU cluster is now officially launched following a VIP event and University-wide information sessions last month. Speakers at the event included Vice-Chancellor and President Professor Mark Scott, Chancellor David Thodey, Sydney Informatics Hub’s Data Science Group Lead Michael Lynch, and seven researchers from across the University who are already using, or plan to use, Apollo for their research.
The information sessions attracted 430+ registrants, in addition to the 460+ researchers and 160+ projects already using the platform, showing strong interest in this new capability.

  • request an information session for your area. 

Researchers explore the foundations of robust omics studies

Omics technologies can generate vast and complex datasets, but the quality of the insights they produce depends heavily on decisions made long before any data is collected.
The Sydney Informatics Hub Bioinformatics team recently delivered a full-day BIO101 workshop on “Design foundations for omics studies”. Researchers from across the University gathered to learn how to prepare omics experiments from the very beginning, to ensure their bioinformatics analyses are reliable, and interpretable. Through practical case studies, group discussions and interactive activities, participants worked through common study design challenges and considered how to make their research more robust, interpretable and generalisable.  
The materials are available online as a reference for your own study designs. 
This workshop is the first in an upcoming series for onboarding researchers across bioinformatics platforms and analyses. To suggest future training topics that would benefit your research group, please email us at sih.info@sydney.edu.au!

Image X team wins global radiotherapy challenge with Apollo!    

An Image X Institute team — Chen Cheng, David Waddington, Emily Hewson, James Grover and Michael Ferraro — has placed first in both photon dose calculation categories of the international DoseRAD2026 Grand Challenge. The challenge drew 46 teams and 565 algorithm submissions worldwide, ranking entries on standardised measures of accuracy and speed. The Image X team's deep learning model, which predicts radiation dose deposition from a patient's medical image and treatment beam data, delivered the competition's best combined accuracy-speed result across both categories, calculating a full treatment's dose in just 23 seconds on a standard mid-range GPU. And to top it off, the model was trained on our Apollo GPU cluster!
For lead researcher Chen Cheng, whose work focuses on real-time patient motion monitoring during radiotherapy, the win validates a broader push toward real-time dose-guided treatment. "This challenge provided a rare opportunity to compare our method fairly against international teams using a common benchmark", Chen said.

Generative AI hands-on Guides    

Developed with HDRs in partnership with the Researcher Development Unit.
The Researcher Hub’s new Gen AI in Research section brings together guidelines and case studies to help researchers make informed, responsible choices about where AI can support their work, and where their human expertise needs to take precedence. A collaboration between Sydney Informatics Hub and the Researcher Development Unit, the development of these pages brought together staff and researchers from across faculties, career stages and backgrounds to ensure the section was built with researchers, for researchers, and grounded in the realities of research practice. 
The section covers: 
— Working with academic literature  
— Communicating your research 
— Writing with AI 
— Ethical AI use in research 
— Planning with AI 
— Transcription and translation  
— Coding with AI 
— Using AI for learning 
 
Each page incudes example prompts, cautions, guidance on disclosure and responsible use, and links to relevant University policies and resources.  

Introducing Rosetta: Research-grade Transcription and Translation App

Fully on-premises on the Apollo GPU Cluster
The Sydney Informatics Hub has developed Rosetta, an LLM-powered transcription and translation application for research audio deployed on premises on the University's Apollo GPU cluster. Rosetta uses WhisperX for transcription in English and 99 other languages, with optional speaker recognition for interviews or meetings. Non-English audio can also be translated into English within the same pipeline. 

Features: 

  • Secure, GPU-accelerated transcription, speaker recognition and translation for research audio 

  • AI-powered transcription in 99 languages using WhisperX 

  • Optional speaker labels for interviews or meetings 

  • Translation of non-English audio into English 

  • Suitable for protected research data, with support for highly protected data expected by Q1, 2027.

Demystifying statistical modelling    

13 October Hybrid workshops: in-person or Zoom

Do you get confused about the differences between linear and logistic regression or ANOVA, ANCOVA, repeated measures and mixed models? On October 13 we will deliver three workshops from our modular statistical training system to demystify these terms and show how all of these align in the one Linear Models framework. Linear Models Module 1 explains how the common analyses of regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA and repeated measures can all be done using the same Linear Models workflow. Module 2 then extends this to the Generalised Linear Model (GLM) framework for non-normal distributions such as Logistic and Count/Poisson regression. Aligning all these different methods into the same box with the same generic workflow makes it much easier to understand and remember how to apply them, while the practical examples provided give clear instructions for later use. The final module on the day, Statistical Model Building, explores the issues around more complex statistical modelling to build multivariable models with many predictors.
