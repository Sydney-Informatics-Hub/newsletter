Core Facility Spotlight: The Sydney Informatics Hub

The Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) is one of the University’s Core Research Facilities. SIH empowers and enables researchers to use and manage their data to answer complex and profound research questions.  Find out more about SIH’s diverse and extensive capabilities in a new video, created by SIH members Henry Lydecker, Cameron Fong, and Alex Shaw. 

 

 

Project Spotlight: Using artificial intelligence to identify weeds 

A collaborative research team including the Australian Centre for Field Robotics, the Precision Weed Control Group, and project support from the Sydney Informatics Hub at the University of Sydney, led by Associate Professor Michael Walsh and funded by the Grains Research Development Corporation,  are developing new and innovative weed control strategies combining robotics, artificial intelligence, and high tech engineering solutions to help the agricultural industry more efficiently control weeds. A key outcome from this project is weed-ai.sydney.edu.au, an open source, searchable, weeds image data platform designed to facilitate the research and development of machine learning algorithms for weed recognition in cropping systems. Dr Henry Lydecker, an SIH data scientist, will be presenting about the development of Weed-AI at Smart Sensing: Developing your Business with the University of Sydney, an event hosted by the NSW Smart Sensing Network at the Sydney Startup Hub. This event showcases how startups and businesses working with smart sensing technologies can benefit from working with researchers and facilities at the University of Sydney. 

 

Strengthening industry connections with targeted Python training

Sydney Informatics Hub, in collaboration with the Petroleum Exploration Society of Australia (PESA), has delivered a 4 session Introduction to Python for Geoscience course to a cohort of researchers, students, and industry professionals around the globe. Delivered virtually over 4 weeks, the targeted training has upskilled professionals in modern approaches to Python programming, including machine learning analysis, cloud computing, and various applications to the broad spectrum of geo-data.  These hands-on interactive workshops expand the cross-industry knowledge-sharing between our staff and the experts working on external problems. Contact us to collaboratively develop and deliver world-class technical training for your industry. 

 

Wearable Technology Research Community Meetup

Wearable technologies are increasingly worn by consumers and provide a vital source of information for researchers. In April, the Sydney Informatics Hub hosted the first Wearable Technology Research Community Meetup. We brought together the diverse community of researchers using wearable devices to study participant behaviour in clinical trials at the University of Sydney, spanning diabetes, architecture, psychology, sleep and other disciplines. By sharing knowledge and addressing common roadblocks, the community hopes to accelerate the time to science from research question to real world application. Prof. Anthony Harris presented how he is using wearables devices to improve youth mental health in his study, UnWired. If you are interested in being a part of the next meetup, contact Dr. Kristian Maras or Dr. Gordon McDonald.

 

Data legislation: will sensitive government data be shared more widely with researchers?

A long-awaited piece of legislation that will make sensitive government datasets more accessible to researchers is under parliamentary review. Tim Payne (Director, Higher Education Policy & Projects) and Adele Haythornthwaite (Research Data Consulting Lead, SIH) were invited to attend (on behalf of the University) a public hearing of the Senate Finance & Public Administration Legislative Committee into the Data Availability and Transparency Bill 2020. 

Our representatives responded to questions about security of data and systems, protection of sensitive data and privacy, and were given the opportunity to discuss several issues, including the importance of government datasets for public benefit research. The legislation returns to the House of Representatives for further consideration shortly.

 

SIH HPC Allocation Scheme, 2021, Round 2 

The SIH HPC Allocation Scheme, 2021, Round 2 closes this Friday 21st May 2021. 

This scheme provides University of Sydney researchers access to time on the NCI Gadi supercomputer, the largest National HPC facility. A total of 15 million service units (MSU) is available per quarter in 2021 through this scheme and will be allocated according to project merit and on a reasonable fair-share basis. This scheme is supported by the DVC (Research) and the ARC. Round 2 will provide access to the NCI Gadi HPC Facility for Quarter 3 (July – Sept) and Quarter 4 (Oct - Dec), 2021.
