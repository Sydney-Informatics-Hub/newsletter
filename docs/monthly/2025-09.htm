A Closer Look at Saying Goodbye: New Research on Pet Euthanasia

Anyone who has ever owned a pet knows how tough it is to say goodbye. And for the vets and veterinary teams who perform euthanasia, it's an emotional experience too.
Recognizing a gap in documented veterinary euthanasia techniques, Dr. Anne Quain led a comprehensive study of Australian veterinarians regarding their routine and emergency euthanasia procedures.
The analyses, supported by SIH’s Senior Statistical Consultant, Dr Kathrin Schemann, benchmarked how almost 700 Australian vets are performing euthanasia of dogs and cats in routine and emergency practice. The findings, featured in Vet Practice Magazine, reveal significant variations in practice based on veterinarian gender and location (metropolitan, regional, rural). The study highlights evolving expectations around euthanasia – not just from pet owners, but from the veterinary teams themselves.
The research informs benchmarking for current vets and training of future veterinarians and offers valuable insights to understand and support the work of veterinary professionals.

Survey Design & Analysis day 

10 September Hybrid workshops: in-person or Zoom

Thanks to university-supported online platforms like Redcap and Qualtrics, delivering surveys to research participants is easier than ever. Asking the right questions within your surveys can be more of a challenge. Is it easy for your participants to understand what you are asking? Will analysis of the responses help you answer your research questions? Is your survey well designed?
On September 10, Sydney Informatics Hub presents a day of workshops relevant to researchers who want to upskill in survey techniques. Each workshop is 90 minutes long and provides practical advice on methods used frequently in survey design and analysis. Surveys I covers design and analysis considerations common to most surveys. Surveys II delves into more advanced topics including assessing reliability and creating new indices using multiple questions.
Register now for one or both surveys workshops to ensure that your next survey will yield valuable information for your research.

Getting started with
spatial omics: webinar and hands-on workshop 

September & October: Zoom

Spatial omics provides unprecedented opportunities for the understanding of cells, tissues and systems by combining omics and imaging technologies.
Join SIH bioinformaticians Fred Jaya and Dr. Michael Geaghan for an introduction to the emerging technologies in spatial omics. In collaboration with the Australian BioCommons and the Queensland Cyber Infrastructure Foundation (QCIF), this two-part, online series includes an introductory webinar and a hands-on workshop for life scientists interested in integrating spatial omics for their research.
Webinar: 10th September 2025, 12 - 1 pm AEST
This webinar provides a starting point from which to navigate the evolving field of spatial omics and the numerous methods available. We’ll begin with an overview of spatial omics and how it differs from other omics methods. We then explore considerations for analysis of spatial omics data, compare and contrast available tools and delve into some of the theory behind the bioinformatics. Registration and more information can be found here.
Workshop: 28-29 October 2025, 1 - 4 pm AEST
This hands-on workshop will show you how to analyse data from in situ spatial (e.g. CosMx, Xenium) experiments with Seurat in R to visualise gene expression within tissue samples. Using real-life experimental data we step through the process of reading in data, quality control, filtering, dimensionality reduction, visualisation and differential expression analysis. We will discuss the ‘why’ behind each step and essential best practices for designing and running spatial omics experiments. Registration and more information can be found here.
