SIH Team nominated for Eureka Prize 2026

We are excited to share that the Online Mendelian Inheritance in Animals (OMIA) database, created by Prof Frank Nicholas and currently led by A/Prof Imke Tammen, is a finalist in the Eureka Prize 2026 in the Excellence in Data Platforms category. OMIA's collects genetic variants about simply inherited (Mendelian) disorders in animals, including cows, sheep, pigs as well as more exotic species such as mouflon, tiger, yak and raccoon dog – to name a few. OMIA is used by over 100 000 researchers annually from all over the world.
OMIA was founded over 30 years ago and, since 2019, has been supported by a number of Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) software engineering projects, including modernising the backend of the platform, developing automated variant submission tools, integrating international disease and breed ontologies, and implementing an AI-powered literature surveillance system based on PubMedBERT. These innovations improved OMIA’s scalability, interoperability, and ability to keep pace with new scientific discoveries.
Together, OMIA’s scientific leaders and the SIH team – Research Software Engineers Marius Mather, Tim White, Xinwei Luo, Software Engineering Group Lead Mike Lynch and Data Science and AI Lead Darya Vanichkina - have created a sustainable digital research infrastructure that combines expert curation, advanced software engineering, and artificial intelligence - demonstrating how interdisciplinary collaboration can accelerate discovery and deliver global impact for animal genetics research.  

New service: BioShell, a new gateway to national compute

Recently, Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) and Australian BioCommons launched BioShell, a new ready-to-use virtual command-line sandbox for bioinformatics experiments. It comes with preinstalled software and workflow engines, a software library of over 100,000 tools, built-in support via an intuitive command-line agent, and full administrative control. BioShell supports national bioinformatics training events and research projects. It’s the perfect starter environment for life scientists and clinicians new to the world of bioinformatics-based research.
SIH bioinformaticians, Dr Mitchell O’Brien, Fred Jaya, and Dr Georgie Samaha, specifically built BioShell to allow researchers to focus on their research, rather than troubleshoot environment configuration and software installations.
BioShell is a national service, free to Australian researchers and their students. Find out more and request access here.

SIH Staff Profile: Chris Howden, Statistical Consulting Unit Lead

After beginning his studies in Genetics at Macquarie University, Chris ultimately completed a double major in Ecology and Statistics (Hons). His first professional role was with the BioTrack lab helping commercialise a faster, more cost efficient biodiversity assessment system that combined emerging information technologies with advanced taxonomic methods. He then joined the Department of Land & Water Conservation where he developed tools to assess and establish environmental flows for river systems. Later, he led the Advanced Analytics team at Colmar Brunton as they applied statistical modelling and data science to inform evidence-based government policy and commercial decision-making. After a break traveling though Southeast Asia, India, Nepal and Bhutan he settled in Alice Springs, where he founded the data science company, Tricky Solutions, which he led for over a decade.
Today, Chris has come full circle, returning to academia at the University of Sydney where, as the lead of the Sydney Informatics Hub Statistical Consulting Unit, he’s enjoying upskilling researchers to do higher impact research which helps get them a seat at the table when society discusses its most pressing challenges. His current interests include embedding next-generation causal methods into researchers’ practice and partnering with the Animal Ethics Committee to strengthen the University’s research quality and return on investment—by reducing poorly designed or underpowered studies that fail to detect impactful effects, yet still consume valuable academic and professional staff time, funding, and animal resources.
Outside of work, he enjoys spending time in nature bushwalking, rock climbing and skiing – both on piste and the occasional backcountry adventure!

Teach AI your codebook: free Wordflow workshop

28 August workshop (2 sessions): Zoom

Working with interviews, social media, news articles, or any other text corpus, but not with Python or R? The Language Data Commons of Australia (LDaCA) have developed a free text analytics app - LDaCA Wordflow - built for exactly that, no code required: concordance, word frequency, trends, topic modelling, quotation extraction, and now GenAI-assisted annotation, all point-and-click, with your data visible at every step.
Join us on Friday August 28 for a free online workshop on LDaCA Wordflow. This online workshop will run as two 90-minute Zoom sessions, and you are welcome to attend half or all of the workshop. No coding experience is required. 
The morning session (11:00am – 12:30pm AEST) will be a watch-only tour, demonstrating concepts, the interface, and a worked-example research workflow built live, recorded for later viewing.
The afternoon session (2:00pm – 3:30pm AEST), will be a hands-on session which introduces Wordflow's new Annotation tool. You will code a sample and define the codebook; the AI must match your expectation before it earns the corpus; then it annotates at scale.
We hope to see you there!
