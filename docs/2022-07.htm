How can we support life science communities? Have your say!

The Sydney Informatics Hub works in partnership with the Australian BioCommons to help life scientists access the bioinformatics resources needed to produce world class research. The Australian BioCommons works directly with research communities focused on specific methodologies including high performance computing, bioinformatics workflows, single cell omics, and metabolomics, just to name a few. These communities host regular, informal meetings and online forums for researchers. They’re a great opportunity to discuss the joys and challenges we experience, and foster knowledge sharing and collaborations. Everyone is welcome!  
For details on how you can get involved, or to propose a new community that could benefit from a shared national bioinformatics infrastructure, take a look at the Australian BioCommons website. The next community meeting is being held on the 20th July for the International Proteomics community.

Staff Profile - Dr Tim White

Tim recently joined SIH as a data science software engineer. He has a background in astrophysics, with a PhD from the University of Sydney, and has worked at the University of Göttingen, Aarhus University, and ANU, before returning to Sydney in 2019 as a University of Sydney Fellow. In his previous research, he measured fundamental properties of stars, and developed data pipelines and novel data processing techniques for NASA’s Kepler and TESS missions. Outside of work, Tim enjoys playing piano, is an avid fan of pub trivia, and is a long-suffering supporter of the Parramatta Eels. 

SIH Scheme Outcomes and Opportunities

National research compute infrastructure: Get help with access

Access to adequate research compute infrastructure is a crucial part of research project planning as our datasets and computational resource needs grow. Gaining access to essential cloud and national HPC facilities can be costly, time consuming, and often requires specialised expertise. High performance computing specialists at SIH support researchers in doing just this. This month, we’re celebrating the successful applications of Sydney University researchers to federally funded computing resources available at the National Computational Infrastructure through NCI’s new Adapter Scheme and SIH’s HPC allocation scheme.
For support and advice applying for similar schemes, contact sih.info@sydney.edu.au.

NCI Adapter Scheme Successes

NCI's Adapter Allocation Scheme provides resource allocations on the high-performance Gadi supercomputer, Nirin cloud, and an interconnected data storage system. Thisis a new initiative to get smaller and weirdly shaped computing workloads on to the National Computational Infrastructure. 
The University of Sydney had 8 successful projects provided with a total equivalent to around 1 Million computing hours allocated. The next call for applications for this scheme should occur around August. More details can be found here.

SIH HPC Allocation Scheme Outcomes

For high-performance computing needs that cannot be accommodated by national merit schemes, the DVCR funds quarterly access to NCI’s Gadi supercomputer. Congratulations to all projects awarded compute hours in the scheme, especially all new projects using Gadi for the first time. We have some exciting science to take place in these “virtual laboratories” and are helping pave the way to be even more competitive in National merit schemes. For all your computational requirements get in touch with us.

Analyzing Longitudinal Data (for linguists and everyone else)

SIH has been collaborating on the Australian Text Analytics Platform, which aims to provide tools and training for all things text analytics. As part of this project, the LADAL data analysis laboratory is hosting webinars on linguistics, data science and computational humanities. The talk is aimed at people doing text analysis, but should be relevant for anyone interested in longitudinal data analysis. Find more details and join via Zoom at the LADAL site.
