Getting started with spatial omics series: Webinar and Hands-on Workshop 

September & October

Spatial omics is opening new possibilities for understanding the molecular organisation of cells, tissues, and systems by combining sequencing and imaging technologies.
Join SIH bioinformaticians Fred Jaya and Dr. Michael Geaghan for an introduction to the emerging technologies in spatial omics. In collaboration with the Australian BioCommons and the Queensland Cyber Infrastructure Foundation (QCIF), this two-part, online series includes an introductory webinar and a hands-on workshop for life scientists interested in integrating spatial omics for their research. 
Webinar: 10 September 2025, 12 - 1 pm AEST
The webinar will provide an overview of spatial omics, compare the numerous methods available, and delve into the considerations for planning your next spatial experiment.
The webinar is free to join but requires registration for a place in advance: Register here 
Workshop: 28 & 29 October, 1 - 4 pm AEST
This online, two-day workshop will guide you step-by-step, on how to analyse spatial omics data in R with real-life experimental data. Along the way, you will be introduced to key best practices and the "why" behind each step.
The workshop is free but participation is subject to application with selection: Register here

Analysing the Time Until
an Event Occurs

22 October Hybrid workshop, 11:30am-1:00pm: in-person or Zoom

Are you interested in understanding the time it takes for a particular event to happen? Whether you're exploring the time until academic promotion, the lifespan of a device, or the durability of a manufacturing component, Survival Analysis offers powerful tools to uncover insights from time-to-event data.
Traditionally used in medical research to study survival after diagnosis, survival analysis is now widely applied across disciplines—from analysing company longevity after financial shocks, to predicting staff turnover, and even determining the optimal time to substitute a soccer player during a match.
To discover how survival analysis can enhance your research, join the Sydney Informatics Hub’s free Introduction to Survival Analysis workshop on October 22, 11.30am - 1pm.
This session open to all staff and students will provide a practical, engaging introduction to survival analysis methods and their applications.

Hacky Hour is tomorrow!

20 August, 2-3pm: Zoom

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup held the third Wednesday of every month where researchers – students, staff, and university affiliates – can gather in a social environment to collaborate and receive research support.
