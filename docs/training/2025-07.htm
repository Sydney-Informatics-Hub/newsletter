Generative AI for Research Workshops 

July and August: Zoom

Sydney Informatics Hub is running a series of online practical workshops designed to demystify AI and get you up and running with using it in your everyday academic work.
These workshops will cover the fundamentals of what modern generative AI systems actually are, which ones you can use, and how to use them for research. You’ll walk away with the skills and tools to implement what you’ve learned immediately. 
What you’ll gain: 
  • Clear understanding of generative AI capabilities and limitations
  • Practical experience with AI tools for academic work
  • Confidence to use AI safely and effectively in your research
  • Hands-on skills you can apply straight away 
The modular series is designed to be progressive, so please attend 101 before 201.

Hacky Hour is tomorrow!

16 July, 2-3pm: Zoom

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup held the third Wednesday of every month where researchers – students, staff, and university affiliates – can gather in a social environment to collaborate and receive research support. 
Hacky Hour is free, informal and everyone is welcome to drop in for advice, or offer their expertise to help others. SIH staff will be available to advise on topics such as high performance computing (HPC), general programming, R, Python, stats and data management. We hope to see you there!

Exploratory Data Analysis and Machine Learning Workshops in R and Python 

August & September: Zoom

Sydney Informatics Hub is running our flagship Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) and Machine Learning (ML) training in both R and Python throughout August and September. These all-day, online workshops are designed to get you up to speed on modern data analysis tools to speed up your workflow and deliver repeatable, reliable results. 
Workshop Structure: 
  • Exploratory Data Analysis: Learn the first steps of analysing your data and getting it in good shape for further analysis 
  • Machine Learning (Days 1 & 2): Supercharge your analysis by using powerful algorithms to find patterns and meaning in your data 
Together, these workshops will get you working better and faster, and set you up with a solid foundation for learning about AI. 
Python Track: 
26 August - Exploratory Data Analysis in Python 
28 August - Machine Learning in Python Day 1 
29 August - Machine Learning in Python Day 2 
R Track: 
23 September - Exploratory Data Analysis in R 
25 September - Machine Learning in R Day 1 
26 September - Machine Learning in R Day 2 

Prerequisites: EDA workshops are beginner-friendly. ML workshops assume basic familiarity with your chosen language but no machine learning experience required.
All workshops will run online via Zoom only.
Register early to secure your place. We hope to see you there!

Our statisticians are coming to Camden!

27 August Hybrid workshops and in-person drop-in session - all welcome!

Sydney Informatics Hub's Statistical Consulting Unit (SCU) are excited to return to the Camden campus on Wednesday 27 August for an informal drop-in session and to deliver three workshops.
Pop into Room 305 in the Centre for Carbon, Food and Water between 3:30pm and 4:30pm to ask any questions regarding your research data, study design or statistical analysis – no registration required.
The workshops earlier in the day will focus on statistical modelling and give you practical workflows for your own analysis. We will demystify the terms linear and logistic regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA, repeated measures and mixed models and show how all of these align in the one Linear Models framework that can be applied to build more complex models with multiple predictors.
See our training calendar for more details and register now.

Explore, Analyse, Discover: Harness LDaCa Tools for Text Analysis 

26 August, 10am-12:30pm: in-person 

On Tuesday 26 August, the Computational Social Science Lab (CSSL) and the Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) will co-host an in-person workshop introducing a suite of text analytic tools developed through the Language Data Commons of Australia (LDaCa), a national digital humanities project funded by the ARDC.
Held from 10am to 12:30pm in the Level 5 Auditorium (Room 503), Moore Theological College, the workshop will demonstrate how researchers can use these open-source tools—available via web and notebook interfaces—to explore text data and generate insightful analytical outputs.
Designed with FAIR principles in mind, the tools are freely accessible and suitable for users with varying levels of technical expertise.
