Getting started with Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) in Python

9 July, 9:30am-12pm: in-person

Data can be messy and navigating its complexities can feel daunting. Every data frame is different, and data cleaning techniques can depend on personal data-wrangling preferences. 
That’s where Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) comes in. Rather than feeling overwhelmed by these unknowns or unsure of what really constitutes “clean” data, there are a few general steps you can take to ensure your data will be ready for your future analysis – no matter what you’re doing next! 
In this brand-new workshop, we’ll demystify EDA and equip you with essential skills to confidently clean and explore your data.  
During this session, we will walk you through practical steps to prepare your data using real-world examples. If you have your own dataset, bring it along, and we will empower you to harness the power of EDA to drive your data-driven project forward.  
Don’t underestimate the impact of EDA - secure your spot and unlock the true potential of your data! 
Please note, this event will be held in-person. However, if attending in-person is not feasible for you, please reach out via email at sih.training@sydney.edu.au to request an online option.
Register now, spots are limited!

Getting started with Machine Learning (ML) in Python

16, 23 & 30 July, 9:30am-12pm: Zoom

Roll up your sleeves and join us for this series of hands-on workshops to understand the fundamentals of machine learning. We’ll guide you step-by-step! 
You will learn how to:  
- Clean, transform, and prepare your data for ML tasks;   
- Explore popular algorithms and build your own models;   
- Assess model performance and fine-tune hyperparameters.
These sessions will allow you to learn at your own pace, fitting the workshop around your own needs. If you have your own dataset, bring it along! You will have the time to translate these new skills directly to your own project.  
16 July, 9:30am - 12pm: Supervised ML - Regression  
23 July, 9:30am – 12pm: Supervised ML - Classification 
30 July, 9:30am - 12pm: Unsupervised ML - PCA  
Whether you’re a developer, data scientist, or curious learner, this workshop will empower you to harness the power of machine learning. Register now, places are limited!

Hacky Hour

19 June, 2-3pm: Zoom

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup held the third Wednesday of every month where researchers – students, staff, and university affiliates – can gather in a social environment to collaborate and receive research support.
Hacky Hour is free, informal and everyone is welcome to drop in for advice, or offer their expertise to help others. SIH staff will be available to advise on topics such as high performance computing (HPC), general programming, R, Python, stats and data management.

Statistical workshops to help you plan your study design and analysis   

1 July Hybrid workshops: in-person or Zoom

Our Foundational Statistical Series is designed to help researchers plan their study design and analysis and thereby to set themselves up for research success.
Research Essentials gives an 8-step generic workflow from study inception to publication that can be applied to any statistical analysis, no matter the analysis method, discipline or software used. By teaching researchers to classify their data into appropriate data types and to use appropriate visualisations to explore their data this workshop empowers them to choose the appropriate analysis method and gives examples of common inferential analyses.
Experimental Design focuses on Step 2 of the 8-step generic research workflow and covers the key aspects of experimental design that researchers and students may need to apply in their research. Topics include sampling, validity, randomisation, bias, blocking and replication. The workshop provides the opportunity to discuss researcher’s own research question and associated experimental design. Register now for these 90-minute workshops on the 1st of July.

'Time-to-event' Analysis - SIH Introductory Workshop 

1 July Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

Across different University of Sydney faculties, researchers are studying the time elapsed up to when a specified event occurs. For example, researchers study the survival of a person after a disease diagnosis or of a company after a stock market crash. Perhaps you are interested in survival time of marriage, time to promotion, how long a device lasts before it fails or becomes obsolete or the optimum time for soccer player substitution during a game.
To learn more about how ‘time-to event’/ survival analysis works, and how you can incorporate it into your research projects, sign up to the free Sydney Informatics Hub ‘Introduction to Survival Analysis’ workshop on the 1st of July.
