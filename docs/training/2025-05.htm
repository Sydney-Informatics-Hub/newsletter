Research computing support: moving from Artemis HPC to NCI Gadi HPC 

After 10 years of service, our Goddess of the Hunt for knowledge (Artemis HPC) will be permanently retired on August 29, 2025. As part of phase 1 of the Sydney Research Cloud strategic initiative, SIH is proud to support researchers migrate their HPC workflows to NCI Gadi HPC, which boasts vastly more CPU, GPU, and storage than Artemis. Under the Sydney Scheme, USyd staff and students can access free compute on this state-of-the-art machine as well as NCI’s cloud platform, Nirin.
Getting started on Gadi is simple, and we have a range of resources to support your migration from Artemis: 
  • Read about the Sydney Scheme and listen to a recorded session overviewing NCI compute platforms
  • Follow our instructions to create an account and project on NCI 
  • Visit the USyd Gadi onboarding guide for a step-by-step introduction to working on NCI Gadi 
There are many support options available to you, so please reach out where needed.
We will be hosting informal half-hour drop-in sessions weekly starting from May 29, 12-12:30pm where you can join us for a chat and get help with anything you are finding tricky about the Artemis offboarding or Gadi onboarding process. 
Join the drop in session via Zoom here. 



Generative AI in Research: Call for Case Studies

We are developing an online resource with case studies of how to do research "things" with GenAI - and we need your help! Please tell us how you're using GenAI in your research via this short survey.
Selected contributors will be invited to participate in a remunerated two-day in-person hackathon/AI co-creation session at the Camperdown campus.  Attendees will discuss, share and collaborate to write about their experiences to develop GenAI use cases to share with the wider research community.
We're looking for concise case studies detailing practical applications of AI across the research lifecycle, including but not limited to: 
  • Literature search, review and synthesis
  • Grant and manuscript preparation 
  • Experimental design and methodology 
  • Hypothesis generation and testing 
  • Data analysis, visualisation and interpretation 
Our goal is to ensure that researchers from across the University of Sydney can responsibly use GenAI tools, to increase productivity and leverage the massive gains this technology enables - while staying safe and within University, journal and funding guidelines.
P.S. Can’t attend the session but have a use case you’d like to share? Please fill out the survey anyway – we’d love to learn and share what our community is doing!

SydneyFMS: Our new software booking platform  

SIH software bookings (for CLC Genomics and Qiagen IPA) are now managed using SydneyFMS (Facility Management System), the University’s new platform for booking research facilities, equipment and services. University staff and students will automatically have an account and can log in to SydneyFMS via single sign-on (SSO). SydneyFMS will feel familiar as it uses our previous system (PPMS) as its base.
The list of facilities using SydneyFMS will grow during 2025. SydneyFMS will ultimately provide a single point of visibility and access to research facilities, equipment and services across approximately 20 different areas of the University. Find out more on the SydneyFMS intranet page.

Workshop: Nextflow for the life sciences

22 & 23 July, 1-5pm: Zoom

Join SIH bioinformaticians, Fred Jaya and Michael Geaghan for a hands-on introduction to Nextflow, a popular bioinformatics workflow management language. Nextflow makes it easy to run data-intensive computational pipelines, enabling scalable and reproducible workflows on any infrastructure.
SIH has been using Nextflow for the last few years to build scalable pipelines for national HPCs and is a member of the Nextflow Ambassador Community.
This workshop will put you on the path to writing your own workflows using Nextflow. You will learn how to use core components to build, run, and troubleshoot a scalable and multi-step workflow. The workshop will be run over 2 sessions across satellite sites across Australia, including from Sydney.
Join us July 22 and 23, 1-5pm AEST. The workshop is free to attend but applications are essential.
Applications close June 27. See the Australian BioCommons website for more details.

Demystifying statistical modelling 

27 May Hybrid workshops: in-person or Zoom

Do you get confused about the differences between linear and logistic regression or ANOVA, ANCOVA, repeated measures and mixed models? On 27 May, we will deliver three workshops from our modular statistical training system to demystify these terms and show how all of these align in the one Linear Models framework.
Linear Models Module 1 explains how the common analyses of regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA and repeated measures can all be done using the same Linear Models workflow. Module 2 then extends this to the Generalised Linear Model (GLM) framework for non-normal distributions such as Logistic and Count/Poisson regression. Aligning all these different methods into the same box with the same generic workflow makes it much easier to understand and remember how to apply them, while the practical examples provided give clear instructions for later use. The final module on the day, Statistical Model Building, explores the issues around more complex statistical modelling to build multivariable models with many predictors.
