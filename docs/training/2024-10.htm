High Performance Computing and Data Storage Training      

30 October, 10:30am-1:30pm: in-person 

Are you frustrated with the length of time it takes to run your code? Learn how to utilise greater resources to speed up your time to science on 30 October with our dedicated in-person workshop covering the University of Sydney’s High-Performance Computer (HPC) ‘Artemis’. Artemis is a service available at no cost to staff and students. The workshop is also applicable to using national HPC facilities and cloud services available at Sydney. 
In this workshop you will learn about using Artemis and Linux systems including directory structure, software, and how to submit and monitor compute jobs using the PBS Pro scheduling software. You will also learn about the Research Data Store (RDS), a place to hold long term secure data, and how to transfer files to various remote locations.

Survival Analysis    

29 October Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

Across the University of Sydney, researchers in different disciplines are studying the time elapsed up to when a specified event occurs. For example, you might be interested in factors that influence survival of a person after a disease diagnosis or you are interested in business survival, staff churn, promotion, or device or marriage failure. To learn more about how ‘time-to event’/ survival analysis works, and how you can incorporate it into your research projects, sign up to the free Sydney Informatics Hub ‘Introduction to Survival Analysis’ workshop happening on 29 October.

Meta-analysis    

29 October Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

Are you conducting a systematic review? Do you want to combine results from multiple studies? Meta-analysis is a powerful statistical technique that can add a valuable quantitative component to your qualitative literature review. Meta-analysis provides a quantitative snapshot of the existing research in your field, enabling you to compare the contribution of individual studies to an overall estimate and understand conflicting results across multiple studies.
Register to come along on 29 October to learn more about how meta-analysis works, and how you can incorporate it into your research projects.

29 October Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

SIH Masterclass: No-code Data Science using Orange

28 November, 3-4pm: Zoom

Have you ever wanted to play around with some machine learning or data wrangling tools beyond Excel, and yet been hesitant to go down the rabbit hole of installing R or Python (or Julia or Matlab or something else... who knows?!?!?...). Then our upcoming Masterclass on No-Code Data Science using Orange is just for you!
We’ll showcase and share workflows for powerful machine learning models and data munging without coding using Orange, an open-source software with an intuitive graphical interface. You’ll learn how to perform exploratory data analysis, visualize complex data, and build and evaluate predictive models without writing a single line of code. Whether you're new to the field or looking to expand your skills, we will provide you with a step-by-step introduction to the world of machine learning and the benefits of using Orange.
Key Highlights (or why we sometimes use Orange at SIH too!): 
  • Rapid Prototyping: Quickly develop and test models to gain insights faster.
  • Connected Workflows: Build sophisticated layers of data transformations that are designed to work together. 
  • Dynamic Visuals: Create interactive visual representations of your data and model results, facilitating understanding and communicating results.
Make complex analyses accessible with another tool in your toolbox!

Join us for Nextflow training at AMSI BioInfoSummer2024   

3-5 December: Hybrid conference 

AMSI BioInfoSummer is a 3-day bioinformatics conference for students, early-career researchers, and professionals hosted at the University of Melbourne, 3-5 December. SIH bioinformaticians will be in attendance, delivering our Hello Nextflow workshop on the final day of the conference. Our workshop will put you on the path to writing your own reproducible and scalable bioinformatics workflows using Nextflow. You will learn how to use core Nextflow components to build, run and troubleshoot a simple multi-step workflow. There will also be an opportunity to hear about the Australian Nextflow Seqera Service. 
There are lots of other activities going on including talks, a poster and fast talk competition, and AMSI Travel Grants on offer for students to attend the conference in person. We highly recommend students and early career researchers apply! See the BioInfoSummer program for more details.

Applications open for Australian Plant Phenomics Network Postgraduate Internship Awards   

Open to all plant-science HDR students currently enrolled with an Australian University, the APPN Postgraduate Internship Awards can enhance a research project with up to $10,000 of access to world-leading plant phenomics infrastructure and data analytical support at APPN Nodes. 
Successful students will work alongside APPN and SIH staff to design and conduct a project, while learning more about using digital phenomics technologies and data analytics. Along with valuable insights for their research project, benefits include career-enhancing knowledge, experience and contacts. 
You can read about recent Award recipients and their research projects on our blog: Postgraduate Interns will explore plant behaviour to improve crops.  
APPN is a national research infrastructure network that provides open access to an unrivalled range of plant raising and phenotyping technologies. Our mission is to accelerate plant research outcomes that resolve agricultural challenges, from increasing yields to mitigating the impacts of climate change on food production and farming methods. Our new APPN node in Narrabri launched in September and is ready to support keen emerging scientists through the APPN Postgraduate Internship Award program, together with world class data support and training via the Sydney Informatics Hub.
Postgraduate students at any Australian university and their supervisors are encouraged to learn more, consult with the APPN team and submit an application. Note that travel and accommodation support is also available if needed. It would be useful to discuss any potential projects with the USYD APPN Node Director, Dr William Salter if you wish to work at the USYD node.
The current application round closes on 30 November 2024. Find out more and download your application form here.
