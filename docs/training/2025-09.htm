Generative AI for Research Workshops

Sydney Informatics Hub are running a series of online practical workshops designed to demystify AI and get you up and running with using it in your everyday academic work.

Research Essentials - Analysing your data

15 October Hybrid workshop: in-person at or Zoom

Do you have some research data but don’t know where to start with your analysis? Was your statistical training a long time ago or you really don’t feel confident about applying your statistical knowledge to your own data? Fret not, but sign up to the free Sydney Informatics Hub ‘Research Essentials - Analysing your Data’ workshop happening on 15 October.
The workshop outlines a generic research workflow from study inception to publication that can be applied to any statistical analysis, no matter the analysis method, discipline or software used. The presentation includes relevant resources at the University of Sydney, descriptive data analysis and show-cases the application of the workflow in examples of common inferential data analyses.

Meta-Analysis

15 October Hybrid workshop: in-person at or Zoom

Are you conducting a systematic review? Do you want to combine results from multiple studies? Meta-Analysis is a powerful statistical technique that can add a valuable quantitative component to your qualitative literature review.
Meta-analysis provides a quantitative snapshot of the existing research in your field, enabling you to compare the contribution of individual studies to an overall estimate and understand conflicting results across multiple studies.
To learn more about how meta-analysis works, and how you can incorporate it into your research projects, sign up to the free Sydney Informatics Hub Meta-Analysis workshop happening on October 15.

Linear Models 3

15 October Hybrid workshop: in-person at or Zoom

Statistical analysis is more than just building the best predictive model, it should also enable researchers to make the discoveries required to build new knowledge. Constructing engaging narratives about their research is also invaluable as researchers look to connect with their field, the community and funding bodies. To do this researchers need to test hypotheses, uncover unknown patterns, and present results in insightful, intuitive and memorable ways. In short, researchers need to build interpretable models. Our Linear Models 3 workshop to be delivered on 15 October explores tips and tricks to make your statistical analyses do just that.
Topics covered will be:
  • Reporting tricks that aid interpretation - estimated marginal means, confidence vs prediction intervals, applying and correcting for multiple comparisons, reporting variable ‘importance’, plus other reporting and interpretation tricks.
  • Model Parameterisation using the Design Matrix - interpreting categorical predictor parameters, dummy and effects coding
  • More on Mixed Models - introducing the random slope.
Check out our training calendar for more information and to register.

Getting started with spatial omics: hands-on workshop  

28 & 29 October, 1:00pm-4:00pm: Zoom only

Spatial omics provides unprecedented opportunities for the understanding of cells, tissues and systems by combining omics and imaging technologies.
Join SIH bioinformatician Fred Jaya and Dr Sarah Williams from the Queensland Cyber Infrastructure Foundation (QCIF) for an introduction to the emerging technologies in spatial omics. In collaboration with Australian BioCommons, this two-day, hands-on workshop will show you how to analyse data from in situ spatial (e.g. CosMx, Xenium) experiments with Seurat in R to visualise gene expression within tissue samples. Using real-life experimental data we step through the process of reading in data, quality control, filtering, dimensionality reduction, visualisation and differential expression analysis. We will discuss the ‘why’ behind each step and essential best practices for designing and running spatial omics experiments.

Hacky Hour is tomorrow!

17 September, 2-3pm: Zoom

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup held the third Wednesday of every month where researchers – students, staff, and university affiliates – can gather in a social environment to collaborate and receive research support.
