[NEW] SIH Masterclass - Mastering Data Visualisation Design

18 April, 3-4pm: online


Data visualization is more than just making plots. It is a powerful way to showcase astounding results, communicate information, tell stories, and influence decisions. But how can we design impactful visuals that are clear, compelling, and captivating? 

In this Masterclass, we will showcase the principles of and best practices for data visualization design. We will explore how to choose the right visualisation type with a goal in mind and how to tailor images for a specific audience. We'll talk about how colour, shape, size, layout and interactivity can make our plots, infographics and charts stand out. 

Whether you're writing your Honours thesis, crafting your first paper or a seasoned researcher with an H-Index greater than 60, this workshop is for you (and just to be clear: this one has no coding involved)!

[NEW] Getting started with Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) in R

2 May, 9:30am-12pm

Data can be messy and navigating its complexities can feel daunting. Every data frame is different, and data cleaning techniques can depend on personal data-wrangling preferences.

That’s where Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) comes in.  Rather than feeling overwhelmed by these unknowns or unsure of what really constitutes “clean” data, there are a few general steps you can take to ensure your data will be ready for your future analysis – no matter what you’re doing next!

In this brand-new workshop, we’ll demystify EDA and equip you with essential skills to confidently clean and explore your data. 

During this session, we will walk you through practical steps to prepare your data using real-world examples. If you have your own dataset, bring it along, and we will empower you to harness the power of EDA to drive your data-driven project forward. 

Don’t underestimate the impact of EDA.

Register now to secure your spot and unlock the true potential of your data!

Introduction to Survival Analysis 

7 May,

9:30-13:30am: hybrid

Across the University of Sydney researchers in different disciplines are studying the time elapsed up to when a specified event occurs.
For example, you might be interested in factors that influence survival of a person after a disease diagnosis or survival of a company after a stock market crash. Perhaps you like to study the survival time of marriage, or you are interested in how long a device or manufacturing component lasts before it fails or becomes obsolete. Survival analysis has also been applied to study staff churn or promotion, and the optimum time for soccer player substitution during a game.

Multivariate Statistical Analysis 1:

An Introduction to Dimension Reduction 

7 May,

11:30am-1:00pm: hybrid

Do you have a high-dimensional dataset (more variables than you have subjects?) Trying to explore it by looking at your raw observations is like being inside a labyrinth. What you see in front of you won’t give you insight into the bigger picture. Enter dimension reduction, which allows you to reduce the complexity of your dataset while retaining the overall patterns that will yield insight into your study population. It may sound complicated but applying it to your research doesn’t have to be. In our dimension reduction workshop, we use simple, real-world examples to illustrate the key concepts involved while avoiding getting bogged down in mathematical detail.  

You might have heard of techniques like PCA and nMDS before but aren’t sure why they are useful. We step you through applying these and other dimension reduction techniques, from raw data to full interpretation of method outputs. Our workshop is an accessible introduction to the topic and a launching pad to apply dimension reduction methods to your own data.
Cut through the complexity and reveal the hidden patterns in your data by signing up for our workshop!

Linear Models 3: Building Interpretable Models to tell your research story  

7 May,

2-2:30pm: hybrid


Statistical analysis is more than just building the best predictive model, it should also enable researchers to make the discoveries required to build new knowledge. Constructing engaging narratives about their research is also invaluable as researchers look to connect with their field, the community and funding bodies. To do this, researchers need to test hypotheses, uncover unknown patterns, and present results in insightful, intuitive and memorable ways. In short, researchers need to build interpretable models. Our Linear Models 3 workshop explores tips and tricks to make your statistical analyses do just that!

SIH High Performance Computing Allocation Scheme 2024 - Round 2 applications now open

  

The Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) HPC Allocation Scheme 2024 provides University of Sydney researchers access to time on the NCI Gadi supercomputer, one of the largest National HPC facilities in the Southern Hemisphere.

Compute resources available through this scheme will be allocated according to project merit and on a reasonable fair-share basis.

If you are looking for more compute or GPU power than Artemis can provide, this is a fantastic opportunity to get on to GADI.
