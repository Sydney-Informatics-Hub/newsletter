SIH Research Project Grant Scheme 

We are excited to announce the University of Sydney Collaborative Research Equipment Grants and NHMRC Equipment Grants Schemes. Researchers at The University of Sydney who held competitive grant funding active on 30 June 2024 are eligible to apply for fully subsidised research support and computing from SIH in 2026.
Up to an estimated value of $50,000 per researcher throughout 2026 is on offer, which could be a combination of:
  • A month’s H200 GPU time (720hrs) on the new Sydney GPU cluster
  • Top-up compute allocation - a bonus MSU on the national supercomputer NCI Gadi on top of your regular Sydney Scheme allocation, or equivalent allocation on Pawsey Setonix.
  • Up to 9 project weeks of expert SIH staff research support in any of our areas of expertise:
    • AI & machine learning
    • Data science
    • Bioinformatics
    • Statistics
    • Associated software development
    • Natural language processing
    • Text analytics
    • Geospatial analysis
    • Image processing
    • Modelling & simulation
    • High performance computing
    • Parallelising & scaling up algorithms 
Half of the available funding will go towards researchers who held an NHMRC research grant active on active 30 June 2024, and the other half can go towards any researchers who held competitive research funding on the same date. Read more and apply under “USYD and NHMRC Equipment Grants” on the intranet here. 
Apply here - Applications are now open and close on Friday 6th February 2026 at 5pm. Outcomes will be announced mid-March 2026.

Machine Learning and Exploratory Data Analysis Workshops in R and Python! 

February: in-person workshops

SIH is running our flagship Machine Learning (ML) and Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) training in R and Python. These all-day in-person workshops are designed to get you up to speed on modern data analysis tools to speed up your workflow and deliver repeatable, reliable results.
Workshop Structure:
  • Exploratory Data Analysis: Learn the first steps of analysing your data and getting it in good shape for analysis
  • Machine Learning (Days 1 & 2): Supercharge your analysis by using powerful algorithms to find patterns and meaning in your data 
Together, these workshops will get you working better and faster than before, and set you up with a solid foundation for learning about AI.

Hacky Hour is next week! 

21 January, 2-3pm: Zoom

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup held the third Wednesday of every month where researchers – students, staff, and university affiliates – can gather in a social environment to collaborate and receive research support.
Hacky Hour is free, informal and everyone is welcome to drop in for advice, or offer their expertise to help others. SIH staff will be available to advise on topics such as high performance computing (HPC), general programming, R, Python, stats and data management. We hope to see you there!

SIH equipped life scientists with skills that scale 

The SIH bioinformatics team closed 2025 by teaming up with Australia’s leading national compute infrastructure providers to deliver two national, hands-on training workshops focused on equipping researchers with bioinformatics skills that scale, supporting the transition from local analyses to running reproducible and efficient workflows on NCI Gadi and Pawsey’s Setonix HPCs.
Our Nextflow on HPC workshop focused on how researchers can scale their analyses by configuring and optimising workflows, with participants noting:
“There was lots of extra detail for customising and configuring Nextflow workflows, so this will be extra helpful when it comes to setting up my own pipelines.”
“Very applicable to my work in HPC and Nextflow pipelines.”
As part of the Australian Bioinformatics and Computational Biology Society (ABACBS) 2025 conference, SIH co-developed Leveraging peak Australian compute to enable workflows for predictive structural biology at scale. This workshop explores how community-driven pipelines can be deployed on Setonix to predict protein structures and their function.
If you’re working in structural biology, we encourage you to join and share your work with the Australian Structural Biology Computing (ASBC). Get involved here.
