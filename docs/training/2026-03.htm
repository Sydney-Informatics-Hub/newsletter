Survey Design & Analysis day

26 March Hybrid workshops:
in-person or Zoom

Thanks to university-supported online platforms like Redcap and Qualtrics, delivering surveys to research participants is easier than ever. Asking the right questions within your surveys can be more of a challenge. Is it easy for your participants to understand what you are asking? Will analysis of the responses help you answer your research questions? Is your survey well designed? On March 26, Sydney Informatics Hub presents a day of workshops relevant to researchers who want to upskill in survey techniques.
Each workshop is 90 minutes long and provides practical advice on methods used frequently in survey design and analysis. Surveys I covers design and analysis considerations common to most surveys. Surveys II delves into more advanced topics including assessing reliability and creating new indices using multiple questions. Register now for one or both surveys workshops to ensure that your next survey will yield valuable information for your research.

Future of AI in Life Sciences 

29 April webinar: Zoom

Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) are increasingly shaping how life scientists analyse molecular data. To better understand how these technologies are currently used and what support researchers need, Australian BioCommons is conducting a national consultation with the life science community.
Join this upcoming webinar to hear early insights from the national review, including emerging trends in AI use, common challenges faced by researchers, and gaps in digital infrastructure and training. The session will provide an overview of how AI is being applied across Australian life science research and where additional support may be needed.
Presented by Dr Minh Huynh and Dr Benjamin Goudey from Australian BioCommons, the webinar will also offer a first look at new training initiatives being developed to support researchers applying AI methods to biological data. These upcoming workshops and resources aim to strengthen national capability in AI-enabled life science research.
The webinar will be held online on April 29 and is open to all. Register for the webinar here.
Help shape the future of AI training by completing the survey and submit your interest for an online focus group. 

Unlocking nf-core: customising workflows for your research 

27 & 28 May workshop: Zoom

Processing large ‘omics datasets presents challenges for many life science researchers, particularly when workflows must be shared with collaborators and scaled across computing environments. Community-developed workflows such as those from nf-core provide reproducible and well-tested solutions for common analyses including RNA-seq, variant calling, mapping, and single-cell transcriptomics.
The workshop Unlocking nf-core: customising workflows for your research will introduce participants to the structure and tools behind nf-core pipelines and show how they can be adapted for different datasets and computational environments. Through hands-on examples using the nf-core/rnaseq workflow, participants will learn how to adjust parameters, configure resources, and customise workflow behaviour.
Delivered online by bioinformaticians Dr Cali Willet and Dr Michael Geaghan from the Sydney Informatics Hub, the two-day training will guide participants through the practical skills needed to run and adapt nf-core workflows. The workshop is aimed at researchers already working with, or planning to use, nf-core workflows in their research. Register today!

Hacky Hour is tomorrow! 

18 March, 2-3pm: Zoom

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup held the third Wednesday of every month where researchers – students, staff, and university affiliates – can gather in a social environment to collaborate and receive research support.
Hacky Hour is free, informal and everyone is welcome to drop in for advice, or offer their expertise to help others. SIH staff will be available to advise on topics such as high performance computing (HPC), general programming, R, Python, stats and data management. We hope to see you there!
