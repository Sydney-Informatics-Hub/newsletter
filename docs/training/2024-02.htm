Demystifying statistical modelling

6 March Hybrid workshops: in-person or Zoom

Do you get confused about the differences between linear and logistic regression or ANOVA, ANCOVA, repeated measures and mixed models? On March 6 we will deliver three workshops from our modular statistical training system to demystify these terms and show how all of these align in the one Linear Models framework. Linear Models Module 1 explains how the common analyses of regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA and repeated measures can all be done using the same Linear Models workflow. Module 2 then extends this to the Generalised Linear Model (GLM) framework for non-normal distributions such as Logistic and Count/Poisson regression. Aligning all these different methods into the same box with the same generic workflow makes it much easier to understand and remember how to apply them, while the practical examples provided give clear instructions for later use. The final module on the day, Statistical Model Building, explores the issues around more complex statistical modelling to build multivariable models with many predictors.

National bioinformatics training survey – have your say!  

Sydney Informatics Hub is a member of the National Bioinformatics Training Cooperative, offering various training events each year to uplift the skills of Australian biologists, clinicians, and bioinformaticians. We collaborate with partner organisations across the country to ensure we can offer the best training opportunities to the life science research community. All our events are online, free, and open to all.
If you’re interested in bioinformatics training this year, please take 10 minutes to fill out our survey, so we can deliver the events you need.

SupercomputingAsia (SCA) 2024 

19-22 February

SupercomputingAsia (SCA) is a major supercomputing conference in Asia, where some of the top supercomputers in the world are located. Co-organised by leading supercomputing centres of the region (including those in Australia, Japan, Singapore, Thailand and Aotearoa New Zealand), and anchored by the National Computational Infrastructure in Australia, SCA24 aims to promote a vibrant and shared high-performance computing and Artificial Intelligence ecosystem, for both the public and private sectors, in Asia.
In 2024, the conference will be held at the International Convention Centre (ICC), in Sydney.
