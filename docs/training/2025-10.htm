Generative AI for Research Workshops

October & November: Zoom only

Sydney Informatics Hub are running a series of online practical workshops designed to demystify AI and get you up and running with using it in your everyday academic work.
These workshops will cover the fundamentals of what modern generative AI systems actually are, which ones you can use, and how to use them for research. You’ll walk away with the skills and tools to implement what you’ve learned immediately.
What you’ll gain: 
  • Clear understanding of generative AI capabilities and limitations
  • Practical experience with AI tools for academic work
  • Confidence to use AI safely and effectively in your research
  • Hands-on skills you can apply straight away

Survival Analysis

22 October, 11:30am-1:00pm Hybrid workshop:
in-person or Zoom

Across the University of Sydney researchers in different disciplines are studying the time elapsed up to when a specified event occurs. For example, you might be interested in factors that influence survival of a person after a disease diagnosis or survival of a company after a stock market crash. Perhaps you like to study the survival time of marriage, or you are interested in how long a device or manufacturing component lasts before it fails or becomes obsolete. Survival analysis has also been applied to study staff churn or promotion, and the optimum time for soccer player substitution during a game.
To learn more about how ‘time-to event’/ survival analysis works, and how you can incorporate it into your research projects, sign up to the free Sydney Informatics Hub ‘Introduction to Survival Analysis’ workshop happening on 22 October.

Workshop: Nextflow and HPC 

18 & 19 November, 1:00pm-4:00pm: Zoom only

Scaling your bioinformatics analyses beyond your laptop? The upcoming Nextflow on HPC workshop will show you how to configure, optimise, and troubleshoot Nextflow pipelines for efficient execution on Australia’s national high-performance computing systems - Gadi (NCI) and Setonix (Pawsey).
This hands-on training covers workflow configuration for HPCs, best practices for optimisation, and strategies for efficient resource use. You’ll learn how to run and customise nf-core pipelines on HPC, and how to write and tune your own Nextflow workflows for performance and scalability. Designed for researchers with some Nextflow experience, this workshop will help you take your workflows from the desktop to national-scale compute systems. Access to Tier 1 computing resources is provided for eligible participants from Australian organisations.
Read more and submit your expression of interest: Nextflow on HPC. 



Sydney Research Cloud: Pawsey Information Session  

31 October, 2:00pm-3:00pm: Zoom only

Sydney Research Cloud is collaborating with the Pawsey Supercomputing Research Centre to deliver a one-hour online Information session introducing the new Sydney-Pawsey Scheme.
In this session, we will cover: 
  • An overview of the research compute landscape at the University of Sydney, including the new initiative offering research compute hours at Pawsey
  • An introduction to the Pawsey Supercomputing Research Centre and its services
  • How to access the Sydney–Pawsey Scheme via the EOI process 
Agenda: 
  • Introduction to the new Sydney-Pawsey Scheme under the Sydney Research Cloud -  Georgie Samaha, Sydney Research Cloud Product Owner at Sydney Informatics Hub
  • Pawsey overview - Sarah Beecroft, Applications Specialist at Pawsey Supercomputing Research Centre
  • Q & A
