GenAI Workshops 2026 - Last chance to attend in 2026

September and October workshops: Zoom

We are running our GenAI workshop series again in September and October. This is the last round of these workshops we are running this year, so register now to secure your place. We had over 1,600 researchers attend last year and 95% of them recommended the course. These workshops give you practical strategies for using AI in every stage of your research, building from the basics of what AI is, right through to how to get the most out of it when analysing your data. 
  • 21 September, 10am-12pm - GenAI 101: Fundamentals 
    Understand how large language models actually work, what their limits are, and how to choose the right AI tool for your research context — including running one locally on your own laptop. 
  • 22 September, 10am-12pm - GenAI 201: Prompting 
    Learn how context and prompt structure shape AI outputs, and which prompting strategies to use — and avoid — depending on your task. 
  • 23 September, 10am-12pm - GenAI 202: Reading & Writing 
    Explore the tradeoffs of different AI strategies for finding papers, reading literature, and writing — so you can use them effectively without being misled by them. 
  • 12 October, 10am-12pm - GenAI 203: Tools 
    Cut through the noise — learn a practical framework for matching AI tools to your research tasks, and leave knowing exactly where each tool helps and where it doesn’t. 
  • 14 October, 2pm-4pm - GenAI 301: Data Analysis 
    Learn tool-agnostic strategies for using AI across your data analysis pipeline — and where to never let it take the wheel. 
  • 15 October, 10am-12pm - GenAI 303: Qualitative Analysis 
    Explore how AI can support qualitative research workflows — and where human judgement remains essential. 
  • 15 October, 2pm-4pm - GenAI 302: Coding 
    From understanding existing code to automating entire projects — learn how to use AI at every level of your coding workflow without losing control.

Demystifying statistical modelling 

13 October Hybrid workshops:
in-person or Zoom

Do you get confused about the differences between linear and logistic regression or ANOVA, ANCOVA, repeated measures and mixed models? On October 13 we will deliver three workshops from our modular statistical training system to demystify these terms and show how all of these align in the one Linear Models framework.
Linear Models Module 1 explains how the common analyses of regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA and repeated measures can all be done using the same Linear Models workflow. Module 2 then extends this to the Generalised Linear Model (GLM) framework for non-normal distributions such as Logistic and Count/Poisson regression. Aligning all these different methods into the same box with the same generic workflow makes it much easier to understand and remember how to apply them, while the practical examples provided give clear instructions for later use. The final module on the day, Statistical Model Building, explores the issues around more complex statistical modelling to build multivariable models with many predictors.

Hacky Hour is tomorrow! 

16 September, 2:00-3:00pm: Zoom

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup held the third Wednesday of every month where researchers – students, staff, and university affiliates – can gather in a social environment to collaborate and receive research support.
Hacky Hour is free, informal and everyone is welcome to drop in for advice, or offer their expertise to help others.

Australian BioCommons Workshop: Long read sequencing for bacterial genomics   

7 & 8 October workshop: Zoom

Long read sequencing has been the go-to method in bacterial genomics, particularly to provide insight into how antimicrobial resistance genes spread throughout a population. The Australian BioCommons are running a free two-day online workshop (7-8 October 2026) led by expert bioinformaticians Michael (SIH) and Magda (QCIF). 
The workshop will cover: 
  • The fundamentals of long read sequencing and how it differs from short read sequencing
  • Hands-on assembly of (ONT) bacterial genomes and identification of plasmids
  • Quality control and pre-processing analyses, and
  • Annotating the assembled genome to identify antimicrobial resistance genes of hospital-acquired infections  
This workshop is free, but participation is subject to application with selection.

The Apollo GPU Cluster is officialy launching this month 
Apollo, the University of Sydney’s new GPU platform for research, officially launched this month, expanding support for AI-driven research, advanced modelling, simulation and large-scale data analysis. 
To introduce Apollo and what it can offer, Sydney Informatics Hub is hosting two online information sessions for anyone interested in learning more about the University’s new GPU capability. 
In the 30-minute session, you’ll learn: 
  • What Apollo is and the types of research it can support 
  • How researchers are using GPU technology in practice 
  • How to get started and where to find support 
Whether you are curious about AI-enabled research, conducting data-intensive research, or exploring more powerful computing options, this session will help you understand what is possible and where to get support. No prior GPU experience is required. 
Session dates: 
  • 17 September, 12:00 pm to 12:30 pm 
  • 23 September, 10:00 am to 10:30 am 
Host: Michael Lynch, Data Science Group Lead, Sydney Informatics Hub. 
Interest so far in the Apollo information sessions has been strong, and places are filling quickly. There are still spots available for this Thursday’s session for those who would like to attend.
