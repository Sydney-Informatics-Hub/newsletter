GenAI Workshops 2026

June workshops: Zoom

Sydney Informatics Hub’s GenAI workshop series returns for 2026. Attended by over 1,600 researchers last year, these hands-on, online workshops give you practical strategies for using AI across every stage of your research — from the basics through to data analysis, coding, and beyond.
  • 1 June, 10am-12pm - GenAI 101: Fundamentals 
    Understand how large language models actually work, what their limits are, and how to choose the right AI tool for your research context — including running one locally on your own laptop. 

  • 2 June, 10am-12pm - GenAI 201: Prompting 
    Learn how context and prompt structure shape AI outputs, and which prompting strategies to use — and avoid — depending on your task. 

  • 3 June, 10am-12pm - GenAI 202: Reading & Writing 
    Explore the tradeoffs of different AI strategies for finding papers, reading literature, and writing — so you can use them effectively without being misled by them. 

  • 3 June, 2pm-4pm - GenAI 203: Tools 
    Cut through the noise — learn a practical framework for matching AI tools to your research tasks, and leave knowing exactly where each tool helps and where it doesn’t.

  • 16 June, 10am-12pm - GenAI 303: Qualitative Analysis 
    Explore how AI can support qualitative research workflows — and where human judgement remains essential.

  • 17 June, 10am-12pm - GenAI 301: Data Analysis 
    Learn tool-agnostic strategies for using AI across your data analysis pipeline — and where to never let it take the wheel.

  • 18 June, 10am-12pm - GenAI 302: Coding 
    From understanding existing code to automating entire projects — learn how to use AI at every level of your coding workflow without losing control. 
Keep an eye on our training calendar for updates - we will be adding more workshops soon.

Hacky Hour is tomorrow! 

20 May, 2:00-3:00pm: Zoom

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup held the third Wednesday of every month where researchers – students, staff, and university affiliates – can gather in a social environment to collaborate and receive research support.
Hacky Hour is free, informal and everyone is welcome to drop in for advice, or offer their expertise to help others. 
SIH staff will be available to advise on topics such as high performance computing (HPC), general programming, R, Python, stats and data management. We hope to see you there!

Generative AI Essentials for Life Sciences  

1 July, 1:00-3:00pm: Zoom

As generative AI becomes an increasingly present tool in the research landscape, using it responsibly and effectively is a critical skill. This interactive workshop is designed for life science researchers who are new to generative AI or looking to enhance their approach.
You will learn to distinguish between commonly used generative AI models and align them to specific research tasks, discuss ethical risks and institutional policy obligations, and address data security and privacy considerations for research data. You will also get hands-on practice writing and refining system prompts tailored to your research area.

Analyse single-cell RNAseq data in R 

28 - 29 July workshop: Zoom

Learn how to perform single cell RNAseq analysis using Seurat, Harmony and BioConductor packages. Discuss the ‘why’ behind each step and work through hands-on exercises covering reading in the count data, quality control, filtering, normalisation, clustering, UMAP layout, identification of cluster markers and differential expression and visualising single cell expression data.
The workshop is free, but applications are required.
