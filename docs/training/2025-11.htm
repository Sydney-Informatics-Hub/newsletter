Final statistical training for 2025!

26 November Hybrid workshops:
in-person or Zoom

Experienced statisticians from the Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) Statistical Consulting Unit will be running our foundational series of workshops on Wednesday 26 November to provide research-focused workshops.
Our 90-minute foundational workshops will guide you through every step from study inception to publication:  

Research Essentials is ideal if you are new to or returning to quantitative research.
Experimental Design will help you plan experiments or observational studies.
Power and Sample Size will guide you through power analysis for your grant application or analysis plan.
While we offer all workshops and consulting in hybrid mode, we’ve found that in-person sessions work best. So come along to Moore College, 1 King Street, Newtown to learn how we can help you plan your study, analyse data, interpret results, address reviewer comments, boost grant success and increase your publication impact.

Hacky Hour is tomorrow! 

19 November, 2-3pm: Zoom

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup held the third Wednesday of every month where researchers – students, staff, and university affiliates – can gather in a social environment to collaborate and receive research support.
Hacky Hour is free, informal and everyone is welcome to drop in for advice, or offer their expertise to help others. SIH staff will be available to advise on topics such as high performance computing (HPC), general programming, R, Python, stats and data management. We hope to see you there!
Please note: this will be the last Hacky Hour for 2025. We will recommence in the new year - please keep an eye on our training calendar for dates!

SIH Showcases Innovation at the ARDC Digital Research Skills Summit 

SIH staff attended the 2025 ARDC Digital Research Skills Summit last month, which brought together experts and practitioners in research skills training. It showcased successful digital skills programmes and resources that work across diverse scales and contexts, and facilitated discussion on key issues for the data and digital research skills community.
Dr Angus Fisk, SIH’s Data Science & AI Trainer, Dr Rose Smail, SIH Data Scientist and our Data Science and AI Group Lead, Dr Darya Vanichkina, presented AI Training: Translating the Technical to the Teachable, sharing insights from developing and scaling our Generative AI for Research programme at the University of Sydney. The talk explored challenges in defining content, navigating institutional guardrails, and creating engaging training.
Dr Mitchell O’Brien, SIH-based Senior Bioinformatics Engineer from the Australian BioCommons, and BioCloud Training and Communication Officer Dr Giorgia Mori then presented Collaborative Nextflow training for Australia’s dispersed research landscape. The presentation highlighted how Australian BioCommons’ distributed training model and national collaborations have enabled Nextflow workshops that connect researchers across the country. By leveraging partnerships with HPC providers and research institutions, and building on the nf-core hackathon, the team developed and delivered hybrid workshops such as Nextflow for the Life Sciences and Nextflow & HPC.
Following this, Dr Angus Fisk ran a hands-on workshop titled Piloting the Copilot: How to effectively use AI for workshop design, guiding participants through strategies to co-create accurate and relevant training materials. The session demonstrated how AI can support the reverse design process, offered practical prompts and techniques, and enabled attendees to test approaches on their own materials, culminating in a shared library of resources and a deeper understanding of AI’s strengths and limitations in educational design.
Finally, Dr Darya Vanichkina and Dr Nathaniel Butterworth, Science Catalyst Programme Manager at Google, led a special interest group titled Unlocking and Accelerating Research Potential with AI for Science Trainers. This discussion focused on the current state, challenges, and future opportunities for integrating AI into scientific research in Australia, fostering knowledge exchange, identifying pain points, and shaping priority activities to build a stronger, collaborative community that empowers researchers to confidently adopt and innovate with AI.
SIH’s participation in the ARDC Digital Research Skills Summit underscores our commitment to strengthening research skills training at the University of Sydney and contributing to national efforts that shape the future of digital research capability and collaboration.
