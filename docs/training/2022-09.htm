Survey Design & Analysis Day

October 5 Hybrid: in-person or zoom

Thanks to university-supported online platforms like Redcap and Qualtrics, delivering surveys to research participants is easier than ever. Asking the right questions within your surveys can be more of a challenge. Is it easy for your participants to understand what you are asking in each question? Will analysis of the responses help you answer your research questions? Are there patterns within the responses that you might not see when looking at the response to each question individually?
On October 5 Sydney Informatics Hub presents a day of workshops relevant to researchers who want to upskill in survey techniques. Each workshop is 90 minutes long and provides practical advice on methods used frequently in survey design and analysis. Surveys I covers design and analysis considerations common to most surveys. Surveys II delves into more advanced topics including assessing reliability and creating new indices using multiple questions. Multivariate Statistics I: Dimension Reduction covers a series of techniques that can be applied to survey data to assess similarity and clustering between survey respondents or examine the relationship between individual survey questions and underlying factors of interest.
Register now for one or more of the surveys workshops to ensure that your next survey will yield valuable information for your research.

Analysing conversation & social media with the ATAP Discursis tool

Online - November 1012:00 pm  - 1:30 pm

The Australian Text Analytics Platform (ATAP) project aims to provide researchers with the tools and training for analysing, processing, and exploring text. As part of ATAP, we've developed a new online version of Discursis, a tool for analyzing topics, concepts and engagement in a conversation.

In this workshop, we'll look at how we can use Discursis to analyse conversation and discourse transcripts. Discursis gives insights into how speakers in a conversation bring up, repeat, and engage with concepts across time, providing interactive visualisations that summarize these patterns. These same analyses can also be applied to social media data. The workshop will be a hands-on session, working with Discursis via an interactive notebook.

This 1.5-hour online workshop will be of interest to researchers and HDR students in Linguistics, Business, Law and Science and others who are interested in working with text datasets - no background knowledge of NLP or coding experience is required.

Are you new to command line bioinformatics?

As next generation sequencing technologies become more advanced and affordable, more researchers are using them to answer research questions across the tree of life. Before we can answer these questions though, there is the essential but challenging task of processing raw sequencing data. The command line can be an intimidating place and piecing together all the necessary steps to process your data can be hard work!

The Sydney Informatics Hub is running training focused on the essential steps and considerations for popular bioinformatics methods, like whole genome and RNA sequencing. Last month, Dr Georgina Samaha presented a popular webinar on mapping and variant calling with whole genome data. You can watch a recording of it and the Q&A session that followed here. Dr Nandan Deshpande presented a webinar with Dr Sarah Beecroft from the Pawsey Supercomputing Research Centre on reproducible bioinformatics workflows and cloud computing. Expect to see it on YouTube soon!

Keep an eye on the Australian BioCommons events page and YouTube channel for more useful bioinformatics training or contact us sih.training@sydney.edu.au.

Modelling Workshops and in-person consultations at the Centre for Carbon Water and Food (Camden campus) 

We were excited to return to Camden and deliver 3 modules from our 4-part Modelling series over zoom and in person! Module 1 explains how the common analyses of regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA and repeated measures can all be done using the same workflow, since they are all based on the Linear Models framework. Module 2 then extends this to the Generalised Linear Model (GLM) framework for non-normal distributions such as Logistic and Count/Poisson regression. Aligning all these different methods into the same box with the same generic workflow makes it much easier for researchers to understand and remember how to apply them, while the practical examples provided give clear instructions for later use. The final module explores the issues around more complex statistical model building and provides suitable workflows. The day finished with face-to-face statistical consulting sessions for Camden-based researchers.
