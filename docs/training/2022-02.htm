Bioinformatics Infrastructure

The Australian BioCommons would like to invite you to a discussion about infrastructure to support bioinformatics workflows in Australia.
We want to hear about your infrastructure needs – the approaches, tools, standards and compute resources you use to develop, deploy and reuse bioinformatics workflows and the difficulties or bottlenecks you have faced. This information will be used by the BioCommons and its partner infrastructures to coordinate national responses to your challenges and put in place fit-for-purpose solutions so that workflows can be developed, discovered, deployed and reused effectively by Australian life science researchers.
We are already working with various research groups and computational providers including NCI, Pawsey, AARNet, ARDC, QRIScloud and commercial cloud providers to better coordinate infrastructure to support bioinformatics workflows in Australia. We are keen to hear from all Australian researchers that use bioinformatics workflows - regardless of experience level or biological domain of focus - to help guide our work.
You can get involved by:
  • Completing a 15-minute survey before the 1st March about your current bioinformatics workflow approaches, tools, standards, compute resources and computational challenges,
  • Joining us for an online video conference on Thursday 22nd March 12:00 - 1:30 pm AEST where we will discuss the results of the survey, and explore ways that national coordination / consolidation could help to address the challenges (RSVP to communities@biocommons.org.au or complete the survey to be invited), and
  • Joining our Bioinformatics Workflows Google Group to keep up to date on future events and activities

Intro to R-Shiny webinar

March 24 3:30 - 5:00 pm

Zoom 1.5 hour webinar

Please join us for the first installment in our "Data Science Short Training" series of 2022, where we introduce R-Shiny.
  • Have you ever wanted to turn your R script into a dashboard?
  • Tell a story with your data that allows readers to dig deeper themselves?
  • Create a website so others can interact with your research data?
Then Shiny is for you - it's designed to build interactive web apps straight from R!
In this webinar, we'll give a broad overview of what Shiny is and explain key concepts essential for understanding how web apps work. We'll also point you to our favourite "next step" resources for developing Shiny apps and dashboards. This webinar is for users who are comfortable using R at least at the level of our introductory workshops who haven't branched out into using Shiny yet. 
A few of our favourite non-SIH developed Shiny apps, because a picture is worth 1000 words for what you can do with Shiny:
  • Radiant, a machine learning, statistics & visualisation dashboard
  • Twitter conference dashboard
  • Development of specific cell types in the mouse nervous system - published genomic data from the author of this newsletter from her past research life
  • Wordle - video series from RStudio about recreating a very popular online word game in R-Shiny

Computational tools to solve your research computing unknown-unknowns

February 25 & 28 Zoom 2 x workshops

Running out of space on your local machine? Spending days running analysis on your laptop? Commuting in the rain to go and use the lab computer? Losing your work from random reboots? There are potentially better ways to approach your research computing!

For the first time in 2022 we are running the Intro to High Performance Computing (for USYD's very own supercomputer called "Artemis"), where we teach you about remote systems, and automating your computational work. The skills you learn here can be applied to any remote computers, the cloud, and Unix style machines (even Mac OSX) - REGISTER (Feb 25th 10 am - 1pm). 
Complimentary to this is the Intro to Research Data Storage (RDS) workshop, here we cover many of the tools and platforms available (externally and via USYD) that can help you manage data transfers, data management, and data sovereignty - including getting your data on and off the HPC described above: REGISTER (Feb 28th 10 am - 12pm).

Flexible HPC and Cloud for Bioinformatics: Introducing NCI’s Adapter Scheme

March 9 1:00 - 2:00 pm

Zoom 1 hour webinar

Omics research is booming, offering us the possibility to understand biology from unique global perspectives. As life scientists increasingly apply genomics, epigenomics, transcriptomics, and proteomics to their research questions, the need for accessible and flexible computational technologies grows. Australian compute facilities are adapting to the needs of life science researchers. In response to the growing demand for flexible access to high-performance computing and cloud, the National Compute Infrastructure (NCI) has launched a new merit-based access scheme: Adapter.
To make the most of opportunities like the Adapter scheme, researchers can benefit from understanding the challenges and limitations of bioinformatics in HPC environments, and how to speed up applications and utilise available compute resources as efficiently as possible. This webinar introduces life scientists to NCI's Adapter scheme and provides insights into preparing a strong application. We will focus on how to test and optimise your bioinformatics pipelines for improved speed and efficiency.
