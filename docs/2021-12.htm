Climate change and Indigenous housing

SIH has proudly supported a multi-institutional research project on Sustainable indigenous housing in regional and remote Australia, led by Professor Tess Lea. This work reveals the challenges in maintaining the habitability due to the climate change and special environmental characteristics on the rural indigenous lands. SIH supported the historical climate data extraction and aggregation in the selected indigenous communities for the past 20 years, and designed visualisations to compare and highlight the increase of extreme climate conditions on the indigenous lands. This work was published as an official Australian Housing and Urban Research Institute report (see also APO, Shelter WA, Australian Property Journal), and received widespread media coverage (The Guardian, SBS, The Urban Developer, NACCHO),  including radio interviews and social media impact. Congratulations to Professor Lea and the team on these great outcomes. SIH is providing further support for extensions of this work, for presentation at the 2022 National Housing Conference. 

 

 

HASS Research Data Commons and Indigenous Research Capability

Congratulations to Professor Monika Bednarek from the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences on her successful ARDC HASS Research Data Commons and Indigenous Research Capability Grant, Developing the Linguistics Data Commons of Australia (LDaCA). LDaCA is led by Professor Michael Haugh, UQ in partnership with Sydney, ANU, Monash, UniMelb, AARNet, FLA, AIATSIS, PARADISEC, ARC CoEDL, Digital Observatory QUT, and CLARIN. The project aims to capitalise on existing HASS infrastructure and continue to rescue vulnerable and dispersed collections such as Indigenous and non-Indigenous languages of Australia. It plans to link analysis environments with these collections as well as new data sources such as the web and social media, to enable new research outcomes and meet the needs of Indigenous communities. SIH, together with the Sydney Corpus Lab and PARADISEC, are supporting Professor Bednarek, the Sydney Partner lead. SIH provides leadership in text analytics applications in HASS and natural language processing technologies, and in developing sustainable digital technologies that are accessible and improve the experience for researchers. LDaCA builds on current collaborative projects including the Australian Text Analytics Platform, co-funded by ARDC, where Professor Bednarek and SIH are delivering powerful and customisable toolsets and training to extract, transform, analyse, and visualise datasets such as speech, written text, web and social media, to gain impactful insights. 

 

Potential for personalised therapy in head and neck cancer

Congratulations to Dr Laveniya Satgunaseelan and colleagues for their recent publication on Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma research. SIH provided support to access National HPC facilities required to analyse hundreds of terabytes of genomic data. The group also used three of our highly efficient and scalable bioinformatics pipelines to process their sequencing data. The mapping, germline and somatic variant calling pipelines are freely accessible via our Github page.  This support enabled the group to understand the underlying causes of this oral cancer in patients under 50 years of age, which is increasing in incidence in this demographic.

 

Dr Rosemarie Sadsad appointed to NCI Advisory Board

Congratulations to Dr. Rosemarie Sadsad, SIH's Research Informatics Manager, on being appointed to the National Computational Infrastructure Advisory Board as one of its three new independent board members! 

Dr Sadsad is responsible for data science, software engineering, simulation, and bioinformatics capabilities at SIH. She is also the Health Informatics/Bioinformatics lead at the Centre for Infectious Diseases and Microbiology Public Health, NSW Health where she leads bioinformatics and technology scale-out projects that support the genomics capability for the NSW Health COVID-19 response and surveillance of infectious diseases. Dr Sadsad is a member of the Communicable Disease Genomics Network and the NSW Health COVID-19 Expert Committee. She is also a lead for developing the Australian BioCommons national bioinformatics platform. These positions provide Dr Sadsad with vast experience of the expanding high-performance compute, data and throughput needs of the bioinformatics and genomics community, all of which will play an important role for the growth and development of NCI in coming years. 
NCI is governed by The Australian National University on the advice of the NCI Advisory Board, which comprises an independent Chair, the NCI Director, one member from each of the Major Collaborators and additional independent members appointed based on their expertise and to broaden the opportunities and role of NCI.

Workshop highlight: Statistical Model Building

In 2021 SIH developed a new Statistical Model Building Workshop. Regression-type models are used across many disciplines at the University of Sydney yet implementing these models with complex real-life data can be a challenge for students and researchers.  Like all SIH statistical workshops, this 90-minute workshop provides software-agnostic, generic workflows that explicitly show how to apply classroom theory to real-life data. It's designed to be accessible to researchers with various subject matter and statistical backgrounds. The workshop has been given three times in 2021 and has been well received by researchers, some comments being: 

  • “Accessible, and FAR BETTER than any other stats workshop I’ve done, I would actually be interested in attending more stats workshops.”
  • “It was good to see stats course theory applied and know how to approach problems generally. That's something I was struggling with and didn't even realise.”
  • “The workflow really helps to approach model building yourself and the references act as a starting point for more in depth self-study.” 

This workshop fits into SIH’s modular statistical training system so we recommend people first attend other workshops such as Research Essentials, Linear Models 1 to 3, or Introduction to Survival Analysis. We run all these courses regularly throughout the year, so keep an eye on the training calendar in 2022.
