Director's Introduction

Welcome to the March newsletter for the Sydney Informatics Hub.  Like the rest of the university, after a long period of working from home we are now starting to return to campus for work. As part of this process we will be starting to offer options for in-person workshops, consults and Hacky Hours. Like all parts of the university, over the past 2 years we learnt so much about the positives and negatives of a flexible work environment.  One of these learnings was that we could provide high quality virtual support and training, and there was huge demand for this.  Due to this, we now believe we are providing access to services for all researchers, especially those not on Camperdown campus. 

Anyway, I hope you enjoy the contents of this newsletter and we are looking forward to supporting researchers both in-person and virtually over the rest of 2022.  

Professor Tom Bishop, Academic Director

Bionic eye research provides hope for people with profound vision loss

Degenerative diseases of the retina cause severe visual impairment and severely impact the day-to-day lives of sufferers. A team of researchers from the University of Sydney and UNSW tested the surgical safety and biocompatibility of a bionic eye prosthetic implant to restore vision. Nine adult sheep had a bionic eye implanted (see this picture of the implant and a human model). Clinical assessment, intra-ocular pressure, imaging, histopathological assessment, retinal thickness and immunohistochemistry were used to assess the implants. The results provided promising evidence of the surgical safety and potential of the Phoenix Bionic Eye to restore a sense of vision to severely visually impaired people. To illustrate these results, the images show healthy retina and blood vessels for one sheep (A) preoperatively, (B) directly postoperatively and at the one-, two- and three-months mark after implantation (C-E). The location of the electrode array is only visible directly postoperatively in Image B due to temporary displacement of the tissue due to the presence of the device.  
PhD candidate Samuel Eggenberger of the University of Sydney’s Faculty of Engineering lead the publication of this work in the highly regarded Biomaterials journal (IF=12.5; ranked number 3 of 90 for Engineering, Biomedical). SIH assisted with advice about appropriate statistical analyses and presentation of results to best communicate the findings to the target audience.

How can we support the life sciences community? Have your say

Join us for an online video conference on Tuesday 22nd March* 12:00 - 1:30 pm AEST to discuss the needs of life scientists deploying bioinformatics workflows in Australia. (RSVP or get more dteails by emailing communities@biocommons.org.au).

SIH Scheme Outcomes and Opportunities

There will be two opportunities opening soon to access the National Computational Infrastructure’s (NCI) Gadi supercomputer, the largest National HPC facility.

SIH HPC Allocation Scheme 2022 Round 2

This scheme provides University of Sydney researchers access to time on the NCI Gadi. Service units available through this scheme and will be allocated according to project merit and on a reasonable fair-share basis. This scheme is supported by the DVC (Research). The 2022 Round 2 scheme will provide access to the NCI Gadi HPC Facility for Quarter 3 (Jul – Sept) and Quarter 4 (Oct - Dec), 2022.  
Current NCI projects supported by previous SIH HPC Allocation Rounds, the Sydney LIEF, or the Grand Challenges Scheme will need to apply to this scheme for an allocation in Q3 and Q4, 2022.
Applications for the SIH HPC Allocation Scheme, Round 2 will be open in April.
For assistance with your application or for more information about the SIH HPC Scheme or other SIH schemes and services, please email sih.info@sydney.edu.au 

NCI Adapter Scheme postponed

The opening of the National Computational Infrastructure’s (NCI) new flexible merit scheme, Adapter, has been postponed until the start of June 2022. The call for applications is expected to open in April. NCI will be offering researchers access to quarterly allocations for HPC, cloud computing, and data storage through the Adapter Scheme. NCI are asking users to register their interest in the Adapter scheme through this brief survey to help them prepare a sustainable scheme that will be available to researchers long-term.   
In response to NCI’s postponement of the opening date, the previously announced Australian BioCommons webinar titled Flexible HPC and Cloud: Introducing NCI’s Adapter Scheme will also be rescheduled to a later date. Dates and registration details will be announced shortly. We apologise for any inconvenience caused. Please contact sih.info@sydney.edu.au for further information and assistance with preparing your application.
