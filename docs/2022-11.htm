SIH Project Scheme 2023: Round 1 applications now open

Applications close November 21st - Apply

The Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) Project Scheme has been developed to invest in and drive excellence in digital and data-driven research across the University by providing grants for SIH support. The scheme provides researchers at the University of Sydney with around 1 to 5 (FTE) weeks of data analysis and modelling. In this round we are focused on supporting researchers with gaining new insights from their data using modern data science approaches, with an emphasis on the following areas of interest:
  • Machine learning 
  • Computer vision (image recognition, object detection, segmentation, pose estimation)  
  • Natural language processing 
  • Models to extract insight from time series data from sensors, for example wearables data 
  • Generative models such as GPT-3, DALL•E or Stable Diffusion.
Projects which will get our attention will have some of the following qualities
  • Do good in the world, e.g. (many people/an ecosystem/an industry/a species/sufferers of a particular disease) can potentially benefit
  • Be a proof of concept enabling a grant application in a larger stream of research
  • Directly enable the publication of high-impact research
  • Utilize our data science skills effectively (see list above)
  • Have the required data ready for us to get to work 
We especially encourage Early Career Researchers to apply as they will be preferenced in this round! 

Preference will be given to: 
  • Early-career researchers (ECRs) seeking to establish a research track record in a particular discipline area; 
  • Mid-career researchers (MCRs) who have not previously used SIH services that are seeking to demonstrate a research track record in a particular discipline area;  
  • Strategic proof-of-concept experiments that will lead to further external funding opportunities 
  • Projects with far-reaching impact, should they be successful.
  • The proposed project must not be otherwise directly funded i.e. proposed research projects must not be within the scope of research that has been directly funded. Access to SIH support for funded research is available at our internal rates. Don’t be disheartened though, because you can request a quote for funded SIH project support here! 
SIH support for projects awarded through this scheme would commence from February 2023, and will typically be 1 to 5 weeks full time equivalent of SIH support for your project, which is an in-kind value of up to $18k. 
Applications are open now, closing Monday, 21st of November at 11:59pm:

SIH High Performance Computing Allocation Scheme 2023: Round 1 applications now open

Applications close December 26th: Apply

The Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) HPC Allocation Scheme 2023 provides University of Sydney researchers access to time on the NCI Gadi supercomputer, the largest National HPC facility in the Southern Hemisphere. Compute resources available through this scheme will be allocated according to project merit and on a reasonable fair-share basis. If you are looking for more compute or GPU power than Artemis can provide, this is a fantastic opportunity to get on to GADI. 
Applications for Round 1 of the Scheme (for resources in Q1 and Q2, 2023) are open now, and close Friday 16th December.

SIH Statistical Consulting talk at NSW Animal Research Review Panel (ARRP) webinar series 

Insufficiently powered studies are unlikely to find publishable impacts. This is not just a problem ethically, as animals have been impacted for no reason, but is also a considerable waste of researchers’ time, money and often the university's Core Research Facilities resources as well. 
The Animal Research Review Panel (ARRP) has oversight of all animal research carried out in NSW, including our own Animal Ethics Committee (AEC). They hosted a webinar on statistics in animal research and invited the SIH’s Statistical Consulting Unit to present a presentation on power and sample size, which was done by Mr Jim Matthews.  Ensuring optimal power and sample size is an important feature within the 3 R’s of animal research - Replacement, Reduction and Refinement. The webinar was well attended with about 150 people online from across NSW.  The ARRP make these sessions publicly available at animalethics.org.au. The session prompted a variety of questions that were answered offline and these are also available on the ARRP website.
For those researchers interested in learning more about calculating optimal sample sizes, power and experimental design we have 2 relevant workshops on 6 December. To sign up please go to our webpage where you can also sign up to our training newsletter.
