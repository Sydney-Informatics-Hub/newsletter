Introducing New SIH Staff

Member: Dr Rose Smail,

Data Scientist

In January this year, the SIH team were very pleased to welcome Dr Rose Smail! Rose holds a PhD in theoretical physics from the University of Adelaide, where she applied advanced mathematics and computational techniques to study the behaviour of subatomic particles. Her research focused on understanding how small corrections, such as electric charge effects and mass differences, impact particle decays, which are key to testing the boundaries of the Standard Model of physics.
Outside of her physics research, Rose has a passion for machine learning, exploring its applications through various personal projects. Now working as a data scientist, she brings her strong problem-solving skills, mathematical modelling, computational methods, and machine learning expertise to real-world applications, using data to uncover meaningful insights and drive informed decision-making.
Rose is excited to engage in projects beyond the realm of physics and to learn about new research areas. She looks forward to collaborating on interdisciplinary projects that challenge her to apply her analytical skills in innovative ways and to contribute to impactful solutions across diverse fields. 

Australian Plant

Phenomics Network Annual Forum 2025 

On February 11 and 12, the 2025 APPN Annual Forum was held at the Australian National University in Canberra. The theme of this year’s forum was 'Infrastructure to Impact'. We delved into our strategy implementation and our growth plans for our state-of-the-art plant phenotyping infrastructure, which will enable research excellence and innovation for sustainable agriculture.
The University of Sydney node had a strong presence at the forum, with six staff in attendance contributing to strategic meetings and insightful discussions with the other nodes from across Australia. USyd Node Director William Salter and Senior Technical Officer Connor Cassidy presented updates from our node, highlighting achievements from the last 12 months and showcasing new projects we will support in the coming year. There was also an opportunity for some team building with an evening boat trip on Lake Burley Griffin followed by dinner with the dinosaurs at the National Museum of Australia.
If you are interested in finding out about the agricultural research and data support services the USyd APPN Node can provide, please reach out via appn@sydney.edu.au. 

SIH Statistical Consulting Unit visits Westmead

Following on from a successful visit in late 2024, the Statistical Consulting Unit from the Sydney Informatics Hub returned to the Westmead campus on February 11 to deliver three foundational statistics workshops and host a drop-in session. The workshops were well received by those who attended, and the team enjoyed delivering the workshops within the state-of-the-art teaching facilities in the Westmead Innovation Centre, Block K.
Responding to the increasing number of students and researchers based at Westmead, the Sydney Informatics Hub will be offering in-person, one-on-one statistical consults at Westmead for the first time. Experience shows that in-person consulting is often more effective than consulting over Zoom, and that many students and researchers are now opting to return to in-person consulting when it is available to them.
In-person statistical consulting appointments will be available on Thursday 13 March, Thursday 20 March and Thursday 27 March with further dates to be made available based on demand. When requesting project help through the Sydney Informatics website, those requiring statistical consulting and indicating they are based at Westmead will be offered an in-person or a Zoom consult. 

Farewell Artemis HPC,
hello NCI 

The Artemis High Performance Computer is planned to be decommissioned from Friday 29 August 2025. New projects can no longer be established, existing projects can continue to use the service as normal. As an alternative to run your research computing workloads Sydney has increased its investment in the National Computing Infrastructure (NCI) via our Sydney Research Cloud initiative for you to transition your HPC workloads and create new projects on NCI facilities.
Come along to a help session to learn how to get access, where to run workloads, how best to transition, and ask any questions you have about the Sydney Research Cloud or NCI.

SIH Staff Profile: Dr Tom Mauch, Research Compute Data Scientist

Tom is a Research Compute Data Scientist at the Sydney Informatics Hub. After receiving his PhD in astronomy from the University of Sydney in 2006 he worked as a postdoctoral researcher in the UK for 6 years, followed by a decade in South Africa working with the team building software for the MeerKAT radio telescope. Tom has a keen interest in processing large datasets for research, building pipelines and implementing novel data analysis techniques.
Since returning to Sydney and joining SIH in 2023 Tom has diversified into a variety of new and interesting projects. A particular highlight was being part of a team that used innovative image segmentation techniques to detect trees and buildings from aerial imagery across the Sydney metropolitan area to measure the urban heat island effect. He has also continued his involvement in radio astronomy research at the School of Physics by contributing to a pipeline that detects transient astrophysical phenomena in multi-terabyte image datasets. He cannot wait to apply what he has learned as an astronomer to an even wider range of research topics at SIH.
Outside of work, Tom is a keen traveler to off-the-beaten-track destinations. He has travelled extensively around Africa and journeyed overland for 7 months from Cape Town to Cairo in 2017. He is also something of a “culture vulture” and has an obsessive love for classical music and will attend (and talk about) concerts as often as he can.

Demystifying statistical modelling 

19 March Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

Do you get confused about the differences between linear and logistic regression or ANOVA, ANCOVA, repeated measures and mixed models? On 19 March we will deliver three workshops from our modular statistical training system to demystify these terms and show how all of these align in the one Linear Models framework.
Linear Models Module 1 explains how the common analyses of regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA and repeated measures can all be done using the same Linear Models workflow.
Module 2 then extends this to the Generalised Linear Model (GLM) framework for non-normal distributions such as Logistic and Count/Poisson regression. Aligning all these different methods into the same box with the same generic workflow makes it much easier to understand and remember how to apply them, while the practical examples provided give clear instructions for later use.
The final module on the day, Statistical Model Building, explores the issues around more complex statistical modelling to build multivariable models with many predictors.
Check out our training calendar for more information and to register.
