IPA software for genomics   

Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) have expanded the number of Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) licenses available to University of Sydney researchers. We now have 5 licenses available free of charge. IPA is a powerful web-based software platform designed for analysing, integrating, and interpreting complex biological datasets. IPA’s highly curated Knowledge Base allows you to identify functional pathways, biological networks, and potential biomarkers.
We are now running the most up-to-date IPA version, which gives you access to great new functionality.
See our website for more information on how to access IPA for your research.

Sydney GPU Cluster - Early Access Update     

We’re ready to welcome early researcher access to the Sydney GPU Cluster – a brand-new AI supercomputer with 24 x H200 NVIDIA GPUs for research use. The cluster is ready for free access for researchers while we are in pre-launch stage.
Please note that the cluster is not ready for storing and processing highly protected data yet.
Since opening early access to the Sydney GPU cluster, we’ve onboarded 25 research projects and granted access to over 130 users. Researchers are already running a diverse range of workloads, including LLM training, photonics simulations, and machine learning in R.
We’re continuing to improve the cluster’s usability and stability as we prepare for the official launch. In the meantime, researchers who would like to join the early-access program can submit a request here.
More info about the cluster, onboarding guide and documentation can be found here.

Help Shape the Future of AI in Life Science Research and Training    

Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) are rapidly changing the way we work in life sciences. To ensure our community is well supported, Australian BioCommons is gathering input to map exactly how these technologies are being applied, particularly regarding molecular data analysis.
We are looking for researchers and support staff at all levels of experience, from those who have never used AI to those who rely on it every day. Your perspective is valued regardless of your technical background.
There are two ways you can contribute: 
  • Add your voice to the survey: Contribute to our national data collection to help identify trends and gaps across the Australian research ecosystem. Take the National AI Survey here.
  • Join a focus group to contribute to our training programs: Share your specific challenges and ideas in a 60-minute interactive Zoom discussion. Register your interest by Friday 13 March 2026. Submit your expression of interest here.
In April, we will share a summary of our findings back with the community in a dedicated webinar. This session will include a first look at our initial training plan, giving you a sneak peek into the workshops and resources we’ll be launching later this year. Register for the webinar here. 

Microsoft M365 Copilot trial – EOI    

Microsoft 365 (M365) Copilot is a tool designed to help us work more efficiently by assisting with tasks like drafting documents, summarising content and generating ideas - directly within the Microsoft apps we already use (Word, PowerPoint, Teams, SharePoint, Outlook etc). Under the hood, it provides access to some of the more powerful online models (OpenAI ChatGPT 5.2 as of this writing) safely within the University ecosystem.
Several researchers were part of a trial last year; however, there are a few more seats available, and we’d be very interested in getting more research-heavy users to put it through its paces and identify what works and what just doesn’t.
If you are interested in being part of this trial, please fill out this EOI form by March 15, 2026.

SIH Staff Profile: Bryn Collyer, APPN Technical Officer

Bryn completed an Honours degree at the University of Sydney in 2024, focusing on proximal soil sensing and spatial mapping of soil properties. He has extensive experience using and repairing soil sensing tools, farm machinery, and electrical components. As a technical officer within the APPN and SIH, Bryn assists researchers with data collection using a suite of plant phenomics tools, including sophisticated drone-mounted sensors, proximal soil sensors, and plant physiological instruments.
Bryn moved to the University of Sydney Narrabri campus in early 2025 and has supported a variety of diverse  research projects in academia and industry. He has really enjoyed supporting large scale agricultural trials this past year, including working closely with APPN Postgraduate Internship Award recipient Harry Paine during the winter cropping season. Bryn worked closely with Harry, a PhD student in SOLES, to collect hyperspectral, LiDAR, soil and root imagery data across his large-scale nitrogen strip trials in wheat, aiming to gain deeper insights of nitrogen’s effects on whole plant growth under field conditions.
As part of SIH, Bryn has benefited from the strong working relationships he has formed with our APPN data scientists based at Moore College. Working closely with Arden and Richard has exposed Bryn to new state of the art approaches to data analysis and this experience has deepened his appreciation for the rigorous standards essential to achieving excellence in data science.

Demystifying statistical modelling 

17 March Hybrid workshops: in-person or Zoom

Do you get confused about the differences between linear and logistic regression or ANOVA, ANCOVA, repeated measures and mixed models? On March 17 we will deliver three workshops from our modular statistical training system to demystify these terms and show how all of these align in the one Linear Models framework. Linear Models Module 1 explains how the common analyses of regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA and repeated measures can all be done using the same Linear Models workflow. Module 2 then extends this to the Generalised Linear Model (GLM) framework for non-normal distributions such as Logistic and Count/Poisson regression. Aligning all these different methods into the same box with the same generic workflow makes it much easier to understand and remember how to apply them, while the practical examples provided give clear instructions for later use. The final module on the day, Statistical Model Building, explores the issues around more complex statistical modelling to build multivariable models with many predictors.

Survey Design and Analysis Day 

26 March Hybrid workshops: in-person or Zoom

Thanks to university-supported online platforms like Redcap and Qualtrics, delivering surveys to research participants is easier than ever. Asking the right questions within your surveys can be more of a challenge. Is it easy for your participants to understand what you are asking? Will analysis of the responses help you answer your research questions? Is your survey well designed?
On March 26, Sydney Informatics Hub presents a day of workshops relevant to researchers who want to upskill in survey techniques. Each workshop is 90 minutes long and provides practical advice on methods used frequently in survey design and analysis. Surveys I covers design and analysis considerations common to most surveys. Surveys II delves into more advanced topics including assessing reliability and creating new indices using multiple questions.
Register now for one or both surveys workshops to ensure that your next survey will yield valuable information for your research.
