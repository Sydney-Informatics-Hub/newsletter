SIH attends eResearch Australasia Conference 

From 20–24 October, Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) staff attended the eResearch Australasia conference at Brisbane Convention & Exhibition Centre. The event brought together researchers and technology professionals to share innovations in digital research infrastructure, data management, and collaboration tools shaping modern research.
  • Dr Gordon McDonald, SIH’s Informatics Team Lead, focused on talent development in digital research infrastructure, in collaboration with staff from Monash University and the University of Melbourne, AARNET, QCIF and the Pawsey Supercomputing Centre, proposing a plan to attract, train, and retain skilled professionals.

  • Dr Darya Vanichkina, our Data Science and AI Group Lead, led a Birds of a Feather (BoF) session on Artificial Intelligence (AI) adoption in research, addressing barriers such as data privacy, ethics, and infrastructure, with colleagues from the University of Auckland, UNSW, UTS, Australian BioCommons, WEHI, Intersect and Google. Together with Dr Cameron Fong, University of Sydney Research Data Consulting Lead, she co-presented on the journey of creating how-to GenAI resources (coming soon!). Dr Vanichkina also provided an institutional view on skills gaps for the adoption of digital research infrastructure, sharing the stage with Catherine Di Vita, Training Coordinator for the Digital Research Alliance of Canada, who provided an international perspective, and an infrastructure provider lens shared by Dr Masoud Rahimi, AURIN’s Lead Data Scientist. 

  • Dr Angus Fisk, SIH’s Data Science & AI Trainer, and SIH Data Scientist, Dr Rose Smail, facilitated a BoF session with collaborators from the University of Auckland, UTS, Hunter Medical Research Institute, QCIF and Intersect on AI’s impact on digital skills training, discussing the imperative to rethink teaching models to meet evolving needs in an AI-driven landscape.

  • Mike Lynch, SIH’s Software Engineering and Research Computing Group Lead, presented work around Digital Humanities collections,  the use of RO-Crates as a metadata container and with colleagues from NCI, Pawsey, UTS and Intersect led a discussion on supporting complex scientific workflows in the era of AI and High-Performance Computing (HPC), sharing best practices for workflow design, automation, and optimisation. 

  • Amanda Zhu, SIH-based Software Engineering Group Lead of the Australian BioCommons, presented efforts to create a unified identity and access infrastructure, streamlining authentication and authorisation to enhance security and collaboration across life sciences platforms. 

  • Dr Sebastian Haan, SIH Machine Learning Scientist, introduced ResearchPulse, a platform leveraging large language models to automate research impact assessment, reducing evaluation time from weeks to minutes while ensuring transparency and adaptability.
SIH’s presence at eResearch Australasia reflects our commitment to advancing digital research capabilities locally at the University of Sydney and ensuring we are aware of (and often at the forefront of) national developments in the digital infrastructure and skills spaces. If any of the above areas are of interest to you and you’d like to know more, please reach out to sih.info@sydney.edu.au to start a conversation.

Dedicated access to Australia’s Setonix HPC for USyd

The Sydney Research Cloud project has secured dedicated access to Setonix, the Southern Hemisphere’s most energy efficient and powerful supercomputer. Researchers will have access to 20 million compute hours per year on Setonix’s advanced CPU and GPU architecture.
SIH is facilitating access through the Sydney HPC Scheme via an Expression of Interest (EOI) process for Q4 2025, proceeding to a broader rollout starting Q1 2026. If you would like to get access to Setonix, fill out our EOI form.
You can find more information on our new partnership with Pawsey on the staff intranet and our technical guides. 

National Curated Collections Platform for Digital Humanities and Social Science 

Work has begun on Curated Collections, a new national web publishing platform solution for digital Humanities, Arts, Social Science (HASS) and Indigenous research data collections. The platform, built on established digital humanities software (Omeka S), will enable easy publishing, importing, and exporting of digital content, migration of legacy collections, and long-term archiving for research teams collaborating across the country.
The project is led by the Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH), in partnership with the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences at the University of Sydney, the Australian Research Data Commons (ARDC), and Systemik Solutions. We have 30 projects at the University of Sydney that have expressed interest in using the platform, and have already identified 2 national and 6 USyd projects to migrate from their existing platforms.
More information can be found here.

Staff Profile: Dr Kathrin Schemann, Senior Statistical Consultant 

Kathrin is the Senior Statistical Consultant at the Sydney Informatics Hub and leads the day-to-day operations of the SIH Statistical Consulting Unit.
She has over 15 years of experience working as applied statistician across diverse sectors, including academia and public health, and also teaching statistics at the university-level. With a strong background working on multi-disciplinary projects in animal and human health (Masters in Biostatistics and a PhD in Veterinary Epidemiology) she brings a practical understanding to research.
Since 2019 Kathrin has substantially contributed to the development of numerous practical learning resources and workflows within the SIH Statistical Consulting Unit that empower researchers across the university to improve their statistical skills. A passionate advocate for incorporating statistical principles early in the study planning process, she especially enjoys helping researchers optimise their study design and analysis plan for higher quality outcomes, reduced resource waste, and increased reproducibility.
Prior to her academic career, Kathrin worked in equine industries and remains an avid horse rider.

Generative AI for Research Workshops

November workshops: Zoom only

SIH are continuing our series of online practical workshops designed to demystify AI and get you up and running with using it in your everyday academic work. 
These workshops will cover the fundamentals of what modern generative AI systems actually are, which ones you can use, and how to use them for research. You’ll walk away with the skills and tools to implement what you’ve learned immediately. 

What you’ll gain: 
  • Clear understanding of generative AI capabilities and limitations
  • Practical experience with AI tools for academic work
  • Confidence to use AI safely and effectively in your research
  • Hands-on skills you can apply straight away 
The modular series is designed to be progressive, so please attend 101 before 201.
