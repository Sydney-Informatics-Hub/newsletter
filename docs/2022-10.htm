marsupial.ai: automatically identifying Australian native animals with deep learning

SIH’s Dr. Henry Lydecker and Dr. Gordon McDonald recently presented to the NSW Department of Planning and Environment’s Convoluted Nerd Network on the work they have been doing on marsupial.ai. marsupial.ai is a deep-learning model for automatic identification of Australian native animals in camera trap images, developed with researcher Dr. Aaron Greenville from the School of Life and Environmental Sciences.  We’re working to save thousands of hours of manual annotation while helping to track population numbers of endangered species in national parks. Try out a live demo of the model, or get in touch if you’d like to use our model in your research. We'd also love to hear from you if you have other object detection or segmentation tasks you’d like to develop a model for!

Modelling workshops and in-person consultations at the Centre for Carbon Water and Food (Camden campus)


We were excited to return to Camden and deliver 3 modules from our 4-part Modelling series over zoom and in person! Module 1 explains how the common analyses of regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA and repeated measures can all be done using the same workflow, since they are all based on the Linear Models framework. Module 2 then extends this to the Generalised Linear Model (GLM) framework for non-normal distributions such as Logistic and Count/Poisson regression. Aligning all these different methods into the same box with the same generic workflow makes it much easier for researchers to understand and remember how to apply them, while the practical examples provided give clear instructions for later use. The final module explores the issues around more complex statistical model building and provides suitable workflows. The day finished with face-to-face statistical consulting sessions for Camden-based researchers.

AgReFed Data Harvester in R: Online workshop, Tuesday October 25th

The AgReFed Data Harvester helps you access geospatial data, enabling reusable workflows for automatic data download, feature extraction and spatial-temporal processing. Data-Harvester supports a large range of geospatial data sources such as weather, remote-sensing, and digital terrain data. We're running a hands-on workshop on Tuesday, October 25th, from 9:30am to 12:30pm, to introduce the tool and show how you can use it in your analyses. This session will be of interest to researchers and students who work in spatio-temporal processing of open environmental and satellite-derived data, especially those who want to join an early-phase open-source project to help develop!

Analysing conversation & social media text with the ATAP Discursis tool

Online workshop, Thursday November 10th, 12 - 1:30pm
The Australian Text Analytics Platform (ATAP) project aims to provide researchers with the tools and training for analysing, processing, and exploring text. As part of ATAP, we've developed a new online version of Discursis, a tool for analyzing topics, concepts and engagement in a conversation.

In this workshop, we'll look at how we can use Discursis to analyse conversation and discourse transcripts. Discursis gives insights into how speakers in a conversation bring up, repeat, and engage with concepts across time, providing interactive visualisations that summarize these patterns. These same analyses can also be applied to social media data. The workshop will be a hands-on session, working with Discursis via an interactive notebook.

This 1.5-hour online workshop will be of interest to researchers and HDR students in Linguistics, Business, Law and Science and others who are interested in working with text datasets - no background knowledge of NLP or coding experience is required.
