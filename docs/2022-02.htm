New statistical consulting service: now available to non-HDR students

SIH is pleased to announce that we have extended our statistical consulting with a user-paid service for those not covered by our free offering.
This new service has already been taken up by the School of Life Sciences (SOLES) and School of Medical Science (SoMS) for their honours students. SIH has provided an excellent value for money service to these schools by providing their honours students with timely and in-depth statistical consulting. This was previously only offered for free to Sydney University academic researchers and Higher Degree Research (HDR) students. The skills gained are invaluable not just for their Honours projects but throughout their research careers. It was a pleasure being able to assist these honours students striving for research excellence with the statistical methods they needed to apply, which are often novel and complex.  
If you are a course co-ordinator for students who aren't eligible for our free service and you would like to explore offering our assistance to your students, please contact Christopher Howden (examples of ineligible students are honours students and non-research post graduate students such as doctors, dentists, and veterinarians). 

NCI Adapter Scheme

Applications will open in February, 2022 for the NCI Adapter Scheme, a new merit-based access scheme that allows for flexible access to high-performance computing (CPU or GPU) and cloud at the National Computational Infrastructure (NCI). This scheme caters for those users who need smaller compute and data resources in short bursts or over a shorter period of time, rather than those who need millions of compute hours over the course of a whole year. Apply for up to 500 kSU per project to be used between April – June, 2022.
The Sydney Informatics Hub will be presenting a webinar (date TBA) in collaboration with the Australian BioCommons, for researchers who wish to apply for the Adapter scheme. We’ll provide you with advice on putting together a strong technical application and show you how to match the needs of your analyses with the compute infrastructure you’re working on. For more details, keep an eye on the Australian BioCommons training calendar or subscribe to the Australian BioCommons newsletter. 
If this is of interest to your computing needs, please contact the Sydney Informatics Hub for assistance with putting together a competitive application.

ARC Funding Successes

Congratulations to all Sydney researchers with their ARC grant successes announced in December. It’s been a privilege for SIH to support many of these researchers towards this great outcome.
We look forward to supporting researchers including Prof. Terry Flew and A/Prof. Tim Dwyer in Media and Communications who are analysing online media and its social value. We will be automating data collection, applying natural language processing and machine learning to help label it for analysis, and developing interactive visualisations that will answer important questions about the future of news media and its role in our society.

Hacky Hour: Next session Wednesday, 16th of February

Hacky Hour is a regular meetup where all researchers – students, staff and university affiliates – gather in a social environment to collaborate and get research support. Experts and mentors from Sydney Informatics Hub and across the University will be available to advise and answer questions on coding, data analytics or digital tools.
Hacky Hour is held on the third Wednesday of every month: our upcoming sessions are at 3-4pm on the 16th of February and the 16th of March. You can join virtually via Zoom.
