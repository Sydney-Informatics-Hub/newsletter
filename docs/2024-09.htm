Introducing New SIH Staff Member: Dr Jean Lee, Data Scientist

SIH recently welcomed Data Scientist, Dr Jean Lee, to the team. Jean is a Data Scientist specialising in Natural Language Processing (NLP) with Deep Learning and Artificial Intelligence applications.
She completed her PhD in Computer Science and Master of Data Science from the University of Sydney. She has worked in various digital innovation workshops (GoogleExploreCSR, NVIDIA LLM Bootcamp) and served in several international AI conferences. Currently, she focuses on Large Language Models, Document Understanding, and Multimodal data in real-world applications.
Prior to academia, Jean worked in the finance sector. She passed the U.S. Uniform Certified Public Accountancy Examination (a.k.a. AICPA) and worked in management consulting firms (Accenture, KPMG). Outside of work, Jean enjoys Pilates, art, and travelling. Welcome to SIH, Jean!

Camden Campus Data Bonanza 2024 

Last month, for the fifth year in a row, the SIH Statistical Consulting Unit (SCU) visited the beautiful Camden campus to deliver three of our software-agnostic, 90-minute, applied statistical workshops and meet researchers face-to-face. Our statistical consultants and researchers from the life, environmental, agricultural and veterinary sciences appreciated the opportunity to discuss statistical issues in the drop-in session.
We received over 100 registrations across our three foundational workshops that provide researchers with an essential workflow to assist in planning any quantitative data analysis, a workflow for the design of experiments and workflows for sample size calculations and power analyses. 
Learn more about the SCU team and the services they provide on our Statistical Resources page. 

Dr. Olga Boichak wins Max Crawford Medal!

Dr. Olga Boichak from the Discipline of Media and Communications has been awarded the 2024 Max Crawford Medal, Australia's most prestigious honor for achievement and promise in the Humanities, in recognition of her research on the nexus between media culture and contemporary wars.
SIH extends our heartfelt congratulations to Olga, a valued colleague with whom we have collaborated for several years, particularly on the Language Data Commons of Australia. 

Visit to the Plant Breeding Institute in Cobbitty

Members of the Sydney Informatics Hub and the Australian Plant Phenomics Network team recently visited Prof. Robert Park's Cereal Rust Research Group at the Plant Breeding Institute (PBI) in Cobbitty. During our visit, we explored their fieldwork focused on safeguarding Australia's food supply from rust pathogens.
We also had the opportunity to meet with engineers from the Australian Centre for Robotics (ACfR), who were testing a self-navigating robot designed to capture rust footprints in the wheat fields. We will be collaborating with our PBI and ACFR colleagues to train an AI model to measure the susceptibility of thousands of different wheat genotypes to rust pathogens. The model will automate the published manual scoring process, also developed by the Cereal Rust Research Group. 

SIH Staff Profile: Hamish Croser, Data Science Software Engineer

Introducing Hamish! Hamish is a data science software engineer at the Sydney Informatics Hub. He completed his bachelor degree at the University of Sydney, focusing on software architecture and design. Before starting at SIH, Hamish spent 2 years teaching within the School of Computer Science, where he also led a number of software projects.
Since starting at SIH a year ago, Hamish has contributed to the Australian Text Analytics Platform (ATAP), collaborating with members of the Language Data Commons of Australia (LDaCA).
Hamish authored and maintains two open-source Python packages: 
- atap-corpus-loader is a general-purpose graphical interface for preparing corpus files for analysis. The corpus loader can be used by research community members to enhance Jupyter notebooks and other Python applications. 
- clause-segmenter is a clause segmenting tool utilising Python's SpaCy. The clause segmenter can be used by Python developers to split a text into semantically meaningful clauses. 
Hamish is currently focused on a project involving analysis of Australian federal Hansard (parliamentary transcripts). The text collection is extensive and offers many interesting avenues for research for political science, history, and linguistics.
In addition to projects at SIH, Hamish has recently created a website that provides information and data visualisations about NSW rental bonds: nswrentalbonds.info
Outside of work, Hamish enjoys board games and taking care of his two cats, Jiji and Roman (pictured).

Multivariate: Dimension Reduction

24 September Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

If you printed out all the data you have collected for your quantitative research study, how many pages would it require? Could you carry it in a briefcase, or would you need a large truck? Increasingly, researchers are working with large datasets, but not all large datasets are alike. Some are ‘tall’ (they contain relatively few pieces of information on a lot of subjects), others are ‘wide’ (containing a lot of information on relatively few subjects). If your dataset falls into the ‘wide’ group, you are likely dealing with a high-dimensional dataset. These kinds of datasets are increasingly common in medicine and health (e.g. high-throughput biology data) and have been collected for many years in other fields (e.g. species abundance in ecology or psychometric questionnaires in psychology). 
High dimensional datasets can be tricky to analyse. Trying to explore them by just looking at your raw data is like being inside a labyrinth. What you see in front of you won’t give you the insight you need into the bigger picture. Enter dimension reduction, which allows you to reduce the complexity of your dataset while retaining the overall patterns that yield insight into your study population. Dimension reduction sounds complicated but applying it to your research doesn’t have to be. In our dimension reduction workshop, we use simple, real-world examples to illustrate the key concepts involved, and avoid getting bogged down in mathematical detail.
Maybe like many others you have heard of techniques like PCA and nMDS, but aren’t sure what they are or how to use them. We step you through applying these dimension reduction techniques from start to finish: from raw data to full interpretation of the method outputs. The result is an accessible introduction to dimension reduction techniques, and a launching pad to explore the applications of these methods in your own research field. Cut through the complexity and reveal the hidden patterns in your data by signing up for our Multivariate Statistical Methods I: Dimension Reduction workshop on 24 September.

Survey Design & Analysis 

24 September Hybrid workshops: in-person or Zoom

Thanks to university-supported online platforms like Redcap and Qualtrics, delivering surveys to research participants is easier than ever. Asking the right questions within your surveys can be more of a challenge. Is it easy for your participants to understand what you are asking? Will analysis of the responses help you answer your research questions? Is your survey well designed?
On September 24, Sydney Informatics Hub will be running workshops relevant to researchers who want to upskill in survey techniques. Each workshop is 90 minutes long and provides practical advice on methods used frequently in survey design and analysis. Surveys I covers design and analysis considerations common to most surveys. Surveys II delves into more advanced topics including assessing reliability and creating new indices using multiple questions.
Register now for one or both surveys workshops to ensure that your next survey will yield valuable information for your research.
