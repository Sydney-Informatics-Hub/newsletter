Project Spotlight: Improving access,  experience and success for Equity students at The University of Sydney 

Our students come from a variety of countries, backgrounds, and life experiences. The University of Sydney is committed through its visions and values to cultivating a teaching and learning environment which is stronger because of the diversity of our students’ backgrounds, approaches to learning and unique perspectives. Our shared educational philosophy commits us all to build “a diverse, inclusive and intellectually exciting environment” for students. 
Senior lecturer Dr Samantha Clarke, Educational Designer Rebecca Denham, Associate Professor Danny Liu and Professor Adam Bridgeman of the University of Sydney’s Educational Innovation team have teamed up with the SIH Statistical Consulting Team Lead Chris Howden and Consultant Dr Kathrin Schemann to inform initiatives and policy development at The University of Sydney. The collaboration aims to improve the access, participation and success of Equity students and to enhance their experiences at university. Equity students are those who might be disadvantaged in some way. For this project we are focusing on students who are from regional or remote Australia, are Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander, or are from low socio-economic status backgrounds, but we are also interested in other equity cohorts, including students with a disability. SIH is enabling the scale and breadth of the analysis, with demographic, enrolment and results data being linked and analysed for nearly 3 million unit of study enrolments for over 90,000 students in 2015-2021.  
Interested to learn more? Look at a snapshot of student diversity and learn some practical tips for getting to know your diverse students to support and embrace diversity in your class. 

Discover and use bioinformatics software across Australian computing facilities

Want to find out which bioinformatics tools and workflows are available at Australian computational infrastructures? Check out ToolFinder and WorkflowFinder, both developed by the Australian BioCommons to enhance the discoverability of bioinformatics software. ToolFinder is a searchable, interactive site that lists all bioinformatics tools installed across several of the BioCommons infrastructure partner systems. Similarly, WorkflowFinder can be used to discover where optimised bioinformatics workflows can be run across national infrastructures including NCI, Pawsey, QRIScloud, and Galaxy Australia. These resources make it much easier to navigate the complex and distributed bioinformatics computational infrastructure in Australia. Happy hunting!

Staff Profile: Dr Januar Harianto - Data Scientist

Before he was a data scientist, Januar worked as an ecophysiologist, modelling the consequences of climate change stressors on marine ecosystems around the world. He studied echinoderms, foraminifera, molluscs and corals, from the San Juan archipelago in the U.S. to the Great Barrier Reef, and Sydney Harbour in Australia. After completing his PhD in 2019, he taught undergraduate data science and statistics while working on the Reef Restoration and Adaptation Program, reconstructing coral reefs in 3D using photogrammetry and extracting spatial information for large-scale ecological modelling. Now, he spends his time developing open-source tools and packages for reproducible workflows in data science.

SIH Scheme Outcomes and Opportunities

SIH HPC Allocation Scheme 2022 Round 2

The Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) HPC Allocation Scheme 2022 provides University of Sydney researchers access to time on the NCI Gadi supercomputer, the largest National HPC facility in the Southern Hemisphere. Computational time (Service Units) available through this scheme will be allocated according to project merit and on a reasonable fair-share basis. This scheme is supported by the DVC (Research). The 2022 Round 2 scheme will provide access to the NCI Gadi HPC Facility for Quarter 3 (Jul – Sept) and Quarter 4 (Oct - Dec), 2022.   
Current NCI projects supported by previous SIH HPC Allocation Rounds, the Sydney LIEF, or the Grand Challenges Scheme will need to apply to this scheme for an allocation in Q3 and Q4, 2022. 
Applications for the SIH HPC Allocation Scheme, Round 2 are now open, and will close 5th June.
