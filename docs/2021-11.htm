SIH at eResearch Australia 2021: Supporting research through technology

The eResearch Australasia conference was held on October 11 – 15th this year. SIH staff presented talks and led BoF sessions around data science & engineering & digital skills. If you'd like a recording of any of these talks please reach out to sih.info@sydney.edu.au

 

Dr Tracy Chew, SIH’s Bioinformatics Group Lead, presented “Scalable bioinformatics workflows for growing ‘-omics’ datasets on National Collaborative Research Infrastructure”. Through our partnership with the Australian BioCommons and National Compute Infrastructure Facilities, we built scalable and efficient workflows that use national high performance compute infrastructure. These workflows have enabled researchers to access Australia’s most powerful compute and analyse hundreds of genomes in a fraction of the time to traditional methods. We shared ways of melding domain best practices with compute hardware through collaborative efforts, and strategies to share these resources more broadly. You can watch our eResearch presentations through the Australian BioCommons Youtube Channel or view the slides here.

 

Dr Sergio Pintaldi attended a “BoF” session leaded by Dr Guru Siddeswara (TERN) about the application of Apache Airflow in research contexts. Many technical users from CSIRO to SodaLabs (Swinburne) showed their user of Apache Airflow to run scientific workflow on terabytes of data. The tool allows them to integrate different data sources and control data manipulation processes both on custom platform as well as cloud and HPC. Sergio presented the application of Apache Airflow in the modelling of climate air pollution to assess health risk in the public and research sectors. The tool would allow the researchers to run their own data pipeline for different modelling scenarios. 

 

Dr Cameron Fong from the Research Data Consulting team presented on “Flattening the curve of RDM support requests: Triaging with REDCap”. Demonstrating the development and implementation of a self-service/triage/booking solution to has made workflows to be more efficient, allowing the Research Data Consulting team to wrestle back control of support requests, consultations and reclaim time for policy, resource and training related work.

 

Dr Darya Vanichkina, SIH’s Data Scientist & Analytics Trainer:

  • Presented on SIH’s work in Improving researcher training through commercial partnerships, discussing modifications to reverse design when working with an industry training cohort and how that benefits university training
  • In collaboration with Intersect Australia - Accommodating high demand for researcher training and support through a partnership between SIH/Intersect in a Go8 environment 
  • Co-led, together with major research training providers Pawsey, Intersect, QCIF, Biocommons & ARDC, the soft launch of the new DReSA portal for training providers (keep your eyes open for a news story about it next year) as part of A national approach to improve discoverability and accessibility of digital research skills training events, materials and trainers
  • Co-facilitated two “Birds of a Feather” events:
    • ML4AU – A community driven approach to accelerate application of AI and ML for research, where approaches to supporting researchers in adopting machine learning methods in their own work were discussed; in collaboration with UQ, Monash & ARDC
    • Training collaborations: Getting specific about sustainable impact, a session around the history and future of Research Bazaar events in Australasia, in collaboration with NeSI, ARDC & QCIF
  • Co-organised the Australian Research Data Commons (ARDC) Skills Summit, rounding up the Skills initiatives: Community call to action.  

 

A highlight of the conference we’d like to share is Professor John Shine PhD AO, the president of the Australian Academy of Science, launching the key findings of the Academy’s Advancing data-intensive research in Australia report, which recommended: 

  • Implementing Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable (FAIR) principles for research data
  • Recognising Data Science as an academic discipline, including through assigning it an independent FoR code, strengthening Australian research in foundational data science, and working towards addressing gender imbalances in the discipline
  • Coordinate and reflect the above in the 2021 National Research Roadmap refresh
  • Ensure that training in foundational data science is accessible to PhD students across all disciplines in Australia 
  • Recognise data generators, curators and stewards when disseminating research outputs

 

Driving up hand sanitiser user in the clinic

The importance of good hand hygiene has been a longstanding pilar of infection control long before the COVID era. A/Prof Denis Verwilghen from the Sydney School of Veterinary Sciences worked with SIH Statistical Consultant Dr Alex Shaw on analysis of a survey about the use of hydro-alcoholic hand rub formulations, known to most as ‘hand sanitisers’. Despite the ubiquity of hand sanitisers, there are a huge variety of different products available to consumers and clinics, with differing formulations, textures, colours and scents. The authors examined how choosing a particular formulation might affect the use of hand sanitisers in the clinic. Respondents, clinicians and students from animal and human health fields, were asked to rank their preference in a blind test of three different hand santiser formulations: liquid, foam and gel. The preferred formulation was liquid, with 50% of respondents ranking it as their favourite. The most important properties for people choosing a favourite included ease of application, drying speed and product texture. Knowledge of the properties that encourage frequent use will help clinics to maintain high levels of hand hygiene, protecting both patients and healthcare professionals in these high-risk settings. 

Identifying drivers for user preference and acceptability of different hydro-alcoholic hand rub formulations. Journal of Hospital Infection Volume 117, November 2021, 17-22
