News and Announcements

Help us shape your future research computing and data analysis service at Sydney   

Last month, Sydney Informatics Hub ran 2 Town Hall meetings to share our vision for the centrally managed research computing service available to all researchers at the University of Sydney. Expected to be launched in 2025, we’ll be replacing our current research computing offering, Artemis HPC. 
We are refreshing our service by lowering the barrier for entry through simplification of access and user-friendly interfaces, delivering new services including Trusted Research Environments, interactive platforms, virtual desktops, and a data movement service. Additionally, we’ll be expanding our relationship with the National Computational Infrastructure (NCI) to provide more computational time to researchers needing peak HPC.
We want to hear from anyone who performs data processing, statistics, computation or simulation work at the University. Everyone’s opinion matters!
You can find a link to our survey and a recording of the townhall presentation on the staff intranet.  

Conservation managers welcome ready-made bioinformatics workflows to support decision making  

The Threatened Species Initiative (TSI) has been working with the Australian BioCommons to overcome barriers that limit the uptake of genetic data into conservation management programs. As a part of their mission, TSI has developed a suite of workflows to facilitate the easy generation of high-quality genetic resources, Including reference genome assemblies. 
Development of these workflows was led by Dr Luke Silva, Postdoctoral Bioinformatician in the Australasian Wildlife Genomics Group at the University of Sydney. Making use of the user friendly ‘point and click’ interface of Galaxy Australia’s Genome Lab, new bioinformatics workflows are now available for anyone to use.
Read more about this effort on the Australian BioCommons website.  

Australia’s new Nextflow Ambassadors connect bioinformatics users to a global community 

Nextflow has emerged as a standard for defining bioinformatics workflows. Now, Australia has two Nextflow Ambassadors to support the researcher community: SIH’s own Dr Georgie Samaha and Australian BioCommons’ Dr Ziad Al Bkhetan. Georgie and Ziad have been awarded positions in the Nextflow Ambassador Program to ensure Australian users are represented on the global stage. The program fosters collaboration, knowledge sharing, and community growth. 
They’ve hit the ground running and SIH will be delivering a Hello Nextflow! Workshop with the Australian BioCommons in September.  
Georgie and Ziad are assessing interest in the formation of an informal Australian Nextflow network that would benefit life scientists, and they want to hear from you!
If you use Nextflow, or are planning to in the near future, share your thoughts by filling out this brief survey. 

Congratulations Sam Rowe!

SIH would like to congratulate our client, Dr Sam Rowe, who successfully passed his Fellowship membership exams for the Australian and New Zealand College of Veterinary Scientists for the Epidemiology Chapter!
We have collaborated with Sam on a project using machine learning to identify intramammary infections in late lactation cows.
This research, currently under review, will allow farmers to use antibiotic-sparing approaches to dry cow treatment.

Introducing New SIH Staff Member: Fred Jaya, Senior Bioinformatician (Australian BioCommons) 

SIH recently welcomed Senior Bioinformatician, Fred Jaya, to the team. Fred specialises in comparative genomics and phylogenetics. He started as a biologist, chasing frogs across the Australian outback, and quickly grew fond of catching bugs in the terminal. Fred has developed open source research software at the Australian National University, and published genomics research applied to ecology and evolution, epidemiology, and conservation. Through the SIH and BioCommons, he is keen to continue sharing his passion for computational research with the broader Australian community. Australian BioCommons Webinar: What exactly is bioinformatics? 

7 August, 12-1pm: Zoom

‘Doing’ bioinformatics to extract, process, analyse, and interpret experimental results is something that all life scientists do as part of their research. But what exactly is bioinformatics? And is there a right (or a wrong) way to do it? Join SIH Bioinformatics Group Lead, Georgie Samaha on 7th August at 12pm to explore common data types, experimental use cases, and some essential - but easy to overlook - practical tips for getting your research done. 
This webinar is free to join but you must register for a place in advance. See the Australian BioCommons events page for details.
