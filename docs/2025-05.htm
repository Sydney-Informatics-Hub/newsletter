Generative AI in Research: Call for Case Studies 

We are developing an online resource with case studies of how to do research "things" with GenAI - and we need your help! Please tell us how you're using GenAI in your research via this short survey.
Selected contributors will be invited to participate in a remunerated two-day in-person hackathon/AI co-creation session at the Camperdown campus. Attendees will discuss, share and collaborate to write about their experiences to develop GenAI use cases to share with the wider research community.
We're looking for concise case studies detailing practical applications of AI across the research lifecycle, including but not limited to: 
  • Literature search, review and synthesis 
  • Grant and manuscript preparation
  • Experimental design and methodology
  • Hypothesis generation and testing
  • Data analysis, visualisation and interpretation 
Our goal is to ensure that researchers from across the University of Sydney can responsibly use GenAI tools, to increase productivity and leverage the massive gains this technology enables - while staying safe and within University, journal and funding guidelines. 
P.S. Can’t attend the session but have a use case you’d like to share? Please fill out the survey anyway – we’d love to learn and share what our community is doing!

In-person statistical consulting at Westmead 

Did you know that the Statistical Consulting Unit (SCU) from Sydney Informatics Hub now offers in-person consulting at Westmead? Alex Shaw from the SCU conducted a successful trial of in-person consulting during March, with the first researcher-initiated in-person consult taking place on March 13 in Block K. As the clinical staff at Westmead can attest, telehealth cannot replace being in-person with a patient, and the same is true when our statistical consultants help you with your research.
Eligible Sydney University researchers and research students can request an in-person statistical consult at Westmead using the Sydney Informatics Hub Contact Us form. Note that non-research students can attend a consult provided another Sydney University-affiliated researcher is present (e.g. an MD student can attend with their university-affiliated clinical supervisor). In-person consulting days at Westmead will be chosen based on demand.
In-person consulting continues to be available at Camperdown and Zoom consulting is available to all eligible staff and students.

SIH Team Featured on NVIDIA Developer Blog for AI Citation Validation Tool  

A dedicated team at Sydney Informatics Hub, specialising in machine learning and AI, attended the Nvidia Generative AI CodeFest Australia and had the opportunity to share insights on developing an AI-powered tool for automatic citation validation. The accuracy of citations is crucial for maintaining the integrity of both academic and AI-generated content. Their project tackled this critical challenge by creating a tool capable of efficiently cross-checking and analysing semantic citation accuracy.
Built using NVIDIA NIM microservices with additional support for mainstream LLM API providers, the system combines a custom fine-tuned model trained on reference datasets with flexible deployment options, streamlining citation checking with speed and precision and making it easier for researchers and editors alike. Introducing New SIH Staff

Member: Dr Michael Geaghan, Senior Bioinformatician (Australian BioCommons)

SIH recently welcomed Dr Michael Geaghan to the team! Michael is a Senior Bioinformatician with SIH. He has had a long standing fascination with biology and genetics, which led him to undertake a lab-based PhD studying the molecular genetics of psychiatric disorders. Through this work he found a passion for bioinformatics and data science.
Prior to joining SIH, Michael was a Senior Production Bioinformatician at the Garvan Institute of Medical Research where he drove the development of several analysis pipelines, particularly for processing single cell RNA sequencing data and for the identification of genetic mutations in human blood samples. Now at SIH and Australian BioCommons, Michael is excited to put these skills to work building bioinformatics tools for the wider research community. Welcome, Michael!
