SIH Research Project Grant Scheme   

Researchers who hold competitive grant funding which was active on 30 June 2024 are eligible to apply for up to $50k value for their research project from SIH in 2026, under the University of Sydney Collaborative Research Equipment Grants and NHMRC Equipment Grants Scheme.
This scheme provides researchers at The University of Sydney with up to an estimated value of $50,000 of research support throughout 2026 for their project which could be a combination of: 
  • A month’s H200 GPU time (720hrs) on the new SIH GPU cluster 
  • Top-up compute allocation - a bonus MSU on the national supercomputer NCI Gadi on top of your regular Sydney Scheme allocation, or equivalent allocation on Pawsey Setonix. 
  • Up to 9 project weeks of expert SIH staff research support in any of our areas of expertise: 
    • AI & Machine Learning 
    • Data science
    • Bioinformatics
    • Inferential statistics 
    • Experimental Design 
    • Associated software development 
    • Natural Language Processing 
    • Text analytics
    • Geospatial analysis 
    • Image processing 
    • Modelling & Simulation 
    • High Performance Computing 
    • Parallelizing & Scaling up algorithms 
    • Machine Learning 
Half of the available funding will go towards researchers who held an NHMRC research grant active on 30 June 2024, and the other half can go towards any researchers who held competitive research funding on the same date.
Read more and apply under “USYD and NHMRC Equipment Grants” on the intranet here. 
Applications open Friday 10 October and close 5pm, Tuesday 4 November 2025. 


NCI data collections 

Researchers working on NCI have access to one of Australia’s largest collections of curated research data, the NCI Data Catalogue. The datasets comprise petabytes of climate modelling, time-series satellite imagery, geophysics, genomics, and optical astronomy reference datasets.
This month, NCI announced the release of the 1000 Genomes Project ONT Long Read Sequencing Consortium Collection (1KGP-ONT).
More information can be found on the NCI Data Catalogue. NCI users can register for access via the MyNCI User portal.

Statistical Consulting Unit visits the Animal Ethics Committees 

Recently Alex Shaw from the Sydney Informatics Hub Statistical Consulting Unit (SCU) sat in on each of the two Animal Ethics Committees (AECs) as an observer. The visit was a continuation of SCU working closely with the Research Integrity & Ethics Administration (REIA) to improve the quality of research involving the use of animals.
The AEC application process requires researchers to carefully consider the three R’s of using animals for scientific purposes: replacement, reduction and refinement. SCU particularly supports researchers in their reduction of the numbers of animals used, with appropriate sample size determination techniques. More generally, through aiding researchers with study design, SCU supports researchers improve the quality of both their work, and their applications to AECs.
With the recent transition of the AEC applications to the new myResearch Ethics portal, SCU is continuing to work with RIEA to make sure our research support resources are readily available and aligned to the requirements of the AEC. Through continuing this dialogue with REIA staff, SCU will develop future resources targeted to AEC-applying researchers including workflows and workshops tailored to performing research involving the use of animals. If you are a researcher considering applying to the AEC, please get in touch for project support.

Staff Profile: Connor Cassidy, Senior Research Technician - Australian Plant Phenomics Network

Connor has been with the Australian Plant Phenomics Network (APPN) based at the University of Sydney Narrabri campus since July 2024. In this time Connor has supported a range of agricultural and land use projects from within the University of Sydney, partner universities, and industry. The research support offered by Connor, through the APPN, includes data collection with cutting edge drone mounted hyperspectral, multispectral, and LiDAR sensors and exploring below the ground with a range of soil sensors such as Gamma and L-band radiometers, or electromagnetic conductivity sensors. Nextflow and HPC: Applications now open 

Earlier this year, the Sydney Informatics Hub ran their introductory Nextflow training in collaboration with partners across Australia. Delivered across Sydney, Brisbane, Melbourne, Adelaide and Perth, this distributed learning model brought together local, in-person nodes with SIH bioinformaticians, Michael and Fred, guiding participants through the fundamentals of building and running Nextflow pipelines. This collaborative approach gave Australian researchers the chance to learn together while connecting with their local bioinformatics community.
Fred Jaya (SIH) and Giorgia Mori (Australian BioCommons) will be showcasing how this dispersed training model ties in with establishing a connected and resilient Nextflow community in Australia at the Virtual Nextflow Summit (23-24 October).
Building on this foundation, applications are now open for our Nextflow and HPC workshop (18–19 November, online). In collaboration with NCI and Pawsey, this hands-on training will show you how to scale and configure your pipelines on Australia’s national supercomputing systems. Apply now!

Meta-Analysis

15 October Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

Are you conducting a systematic review? Do you want to combine results from multiple studies? Meta-Analysis is a powerful statistical technique that can add a valuable quantitative component to your qualitative literature review.
Meta-analysis provides a quantitative snapshot of the existing research in your field, enabling you to compare the contribution of individual studies to an overall estimate and understand conflicting results across multiple studies.
To learn more about how meta-analysis works, and how you can incorporate it into your research projects, sign up to the free Sydney Informatics Hub Meta-Analysis workshop happening on October 15.

Linear Models 3

15 October Hybrid workshop: in-person or Zoom

Statistical analysis is more than just building the best predictive model, it should also enable researchers to make the discoveries required to build new knowledge. Constructing engaging narratives about their research is also invaluable as researchers look to connect with their field, the community and funding bodies. To do this researchers need to test hypotheses, uncover unknown patterns, and present results in insightful, intuitive and memorable ways. In short, researchers need to build interpretable models. Our Linear Models 3 workshop to be delivered on 15 October explores tips and tricks to make your statistical analyses do just that.
Topics covered will be:
  • Reporting tricks that aid interpretation - estimated marginal means, confidence vs prediction intervals, applying and correcting for multiple comparisons, reporting variable ‘importance’, plus other reporting and interpretation tricks.
  • Model Parameterisation using the Design Matrix - interpreting categorical predictor parameters, dummy and effects coding
  • More on Mixed Models - introducing the random slope.
Check out our training calendar for more information and to register.

Survival Analysis

22 October Hybrid workshop: in-person 

or Zoom

Across the University of Sydney researchers in different disciplines are studying the time elapsed up to when a specified event occurs. For example, you might be interested in factors that influence survival of a person after a disease diagnosis or survival of a company after a stock market crash. Perhaps you like to study the survival time of marriage, or you are interested in how long a device or manufacturing component lasts before it fails or becomes obsolete. Survival analysis has also been applied to study staff churn or promotion, and the optimum time for soccer player substitution during a game.
To learn more about how ‘time-to event’/ survival analysis works, and how you can incorporate it into your research projects, sign up to the free Sydney Informatics Hub Introduction to Survival Analysis workshop happening on 22 October.
