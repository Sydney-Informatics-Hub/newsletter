Farewell Artemis HPC, hello NCI  

Artemis is planned to be decommissioned on Friday 29 August 2025. No new projects will be created on Artemis after Friday 28 February 2025.
As an alternative to run your research computing workloads Sydney has increased its investment in the National Computing Infrastructure (NCI) via our Sydney Research Cloud initiative for you to transition your HPC workloads and create new projects on NCI facilities.

Project spotlight: FRONTIER’s new model-based dementia screening tool 

Congratulations to Dr David Foxe from the FRONTIER Frontotemporal Dementia Research Group, who, with support from SIH statisticians, has developed a statistical model that builds on and outperforms the benchmark Addenbrooke's Cognitive Examination-III (ACE-III) dementia screening metric (ACE-III total score). By leveraging patient data from the FRONTIER clinic and using a combination of smaller scale ACE-III metrics, this model improves the accuracy of predicting both common and rarer forms of dementia. It will be especially valuable for clinics and clinicians with less experience in diagnosing these conditions.
This research was presented at the University of Cambridge, where the ACE was originally developed, and is currently being validated with data from clinical researchers at UNSW. Administration of the screening tool is intended exclusively for clinicians experienced with the ACE-III. However, it is available to others to view here.
SIH consultant statistician Omar Arnaiz, along with Statistical Consulting Unit team lead Chris Howden, created the statistical model to identify dementia cases at a higher rate than the traditional ACE-III Total metric. The model identified the specific components of the ACE total score which had the greatest predictive effect and directly compared findings to a benchmark model built using only the ACE Total score. A clinically useful online tool was developed by SIH data scientist Marius Mather, adding to the impact of this research by giving clinicians easy access to this dementia screening model.
Do you need statistical modelling or feel your research could benefit from our assistance? Contact us!

Introducing New SIH Staff Member:
Dr Mitchell O'Brien, Senior
Bioinformatics Engineer
(Australian BioCommons) 

In late 2024, SIH welcomed Dr Mitchell O'Brien to the team! Mitchell's journey into bioinformatics began with a love for animals—especially dogs. While studying Animal and Veterinary Bioscience, he became fascinated by genetics, which led him to a PhD in genomics, exploring complex canine traits and honing skills in data analysis and coding.
Since then, he has worked across diverse fields, from clinical genomics to conservation biology, developing bioinformatics tools that make research more accessible. Through SIH and the Australian BioCommons, he now focuses on building scalable infrastructure and fostering collaboration in the field. 
But the dog days are not over! While his core work spans many areas, Mitchell still pursues passion projects in canine genetics. At home, he’s just as committed—raising a spirited dachshund puppy, who keeps him as busy as his research. 

Introducing New SIH Staff Member: Dr Angus Fisk, Data Science Trainer

Dr Angus Fisk also joined the SIH team at the end of 2024! Angus has taken on the role of Data Science Trainer here at Sydney Informatics Hub. He will be leading our data science training programs as we re-introduce in person workshops here across both Python and R.
He has taken an unusual path into data science, initially training and working as a small animal Veterinarian here in Sydney. He then took on a PhD in Sleep and Circadian Rhythms at the University of Oxford, and learnt to use data science tools to manage and analyse his data there. This means he has plenty of experience of the challenges of learning data science in the middle of a PhD and all the pressure that brings with it! Outside of work, he's usually found on his bike racing with the Northern Sydney Cycling Club.

Statistics workshop day and drop-in session at Westmead Block K in February 

11 February Hybrid workshops: in-person at Westmead or Zoom

Are you in need of statistical support? Experienced statisticians from the Sydney Informatics Hub (SIH) Statistical Consulting Unit will be at Westmead Block K on Tuesday 11 February to provide research-focused workshops and free, one-on-one, in-person statistical consulting. 
Our 90-minute foundational workshops will guide you through every step from study inception to publication:
Research Essentials is ideal if you are new to or returning to quantitative research.
Experimental Design will help you plan experiments or observational studies.
Power and Sample Size will guide you through power analysis for your grant application or analysis plan. 
Following the workshops, you can join a one-hour consulting drop-in session—a great opportunity to ask any statistical questions about your current or future research. While we offer all workshops and consulting in hybrid mode, we’ve found that in-person sessions work best.
Register now and come along to learn how we can help you plan your study, analyse data, interpret results, address reviewer comments, boost grant success and increase your publication impact.

SIH to host Australian outpost of the nf-core hackathon  

SIH hosting the first Australian outpost of the nf-core hackathon, March 24-26th. The nf-core community is a global effort to collaboratively build and maintain open-source bioinformatics workflows. Not sure what the nf-core hackathon involves? Take a look at their pipeline archive and this bytesize talk.
We’re inviting Nextflow enthusiasts who are keen to contribute their skills to the nf-core effort to join us at Moore College for 3 days of fun collaboration. You’ll work alongside Australian Nextflow ambassadors and friends from around the country who will be writing training materials and developing workflows like nf-core/proteinfold.
Our outpost is sponsored by Australian BioCommons, please see our event page for more information. If you have any questions about how you can get involved, please reach out to Georgie Samaha.
