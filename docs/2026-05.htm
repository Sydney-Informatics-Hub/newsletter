When AI Gets It Wrong: Fixing Flawed Research Before It Starts  

Consulting with 500+ researchers annually puts our Sydney Informatics Hub statistical consultants at the coalface of AI use. Increasingly, researchers arrive with AI advice that is catastrophically wrong. The consequences being flawed study designs and analyses incapable of detecting patterns of interest - meaning wasted time & resources across both researchers and university support teams. It often doubles our workload: with the first consult convincing researchers the AI advice was incorrect, and then a second to get them back on track (this being the only one required if AI hadn’t led them astray).
We’ve long had a strategy for answering common questions outside consults, reserving them for deeper enquires and higher impact. Our workshops—built around best-practice statistical workflows to increase statistical literacy —are central to this. To get on the front foot we recently added four slides on using AI effectively in study design and data analysis. They’re now used in both workshops and AI-related consults, with positive feedback.
If you’re using AI for analysis or study design, we encourage you to review what we’ve found works! The key slide is included here, and the rest can be found at the start of all our workshops (e.g. slide 6 of Linear Models 1). One of the key tips is to break your analysis, study design and code into discrete steps based on a trusted workflow (such as the ones in our workshops). Then ask AI succinct questions at each step with sufficient context for it to provide answers from relevant material e.g. domain, research question, type of response and predictor variables, etc.

Text Analytics for Researchers — Free, Open Source, No Coding Required  

3 June, 1:30-4:30pm: in-person 

The LDaCA Text Analytics Tools is a free, open-source application that brings comparative keyword analysis, concordance, BERTopic topic modelling, quoted-speech extraction, and trends-over-time analysis into a single point-and-click workspace. Built by the Sydney Informatics Hub, Sydney Corpus Lab and the Language Data Commons of Australia, it runs natively on macOS and Windows or in the browser at ARDC binderhub — your data stays in a safe environment, and every filter, sample and analysis is captured in a reproducible workspace graph.
This hands-on 3-hour workshop is for HDR students and researchers who work with text — and for qualitative researchers curious about scaling close readings to corpus-level evidence without giving up interpretive depth.
Bring your laptop and a sample of your own data, and get a head start with this powerful one-stop-shop text analytics tool.  
Places are limited - registrations will close at 11:59pm on 31 May.

Generative AI Essentials for Life Sciences 

1 July, 1:00-3:00pm: Zoom

As generative AI becomes an increasingly present tool in the research landscape, using it responsibly and effectively is a critical skill. This interactive workshop is designed for life science researchers who are new to generative AI or looking to enhance their approach.
You will learn to distinguish between commonly used generative AI models and align them to specific research tasks, discuss ethical risks and institutional policy obligations, and address data security and privacy considerations for research data. You will also get hands-on practice writing and refining system prompts tailored to your research area.
